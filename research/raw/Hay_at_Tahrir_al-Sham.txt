'''Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham''' ('''HTS'''){{Efn|{{langx|ar|هيئة تحرير الشام|Hayʼat Taḥrīr ash-Shām}}; {{lit|Levant Liberation Committee}}}} was a [[Sunni Islam|Sunni]] [[Islamism|Islamist]] political and paramilitary group involved in the [[Syrian civil war]].<ref>{{Cite book |last1=Drevon |first1=Haenni |url=https://cadmus.eui.eu/bitstream/handle/1814/69795/RSC%202021_08-EN.pdf |title=How Global Jihad Relocalises and Where it Leads: The Case of HTS, the Former AQ Franchise in Syria |last2=Jerome |first2=Patrick |publisher=European University Institute |year=2021 |location=San Domenico di Fiesole (FI), Italy |page=v |chapter=Abstract |issn=1028-3625 |quote=HTS's domination was followed by a policy of gradual opening and mainstreamisation. The group has had to open up to local communities and make concessions, especially in the religious sphere. HTS is seeking international acceptance with the development of a strategic partnership with Turkey and desires to open dialogue with Western countries. Overall, HTS has transformed from formerly being a salafi jihadi organisation into having a new mainstream approach to political Islam. |access-date=16 January 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230129194309/https://cadmus.eui.eu/bitstream/handle/1814/69795/RSC%202021_08-EN.pdf |archive-date=29 January 2023 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |last=Zelin |first=Aaron Y. |title=The Age of Political Jihadism: A Study of Hayat Tahrir al-Sham |publisher=The Washington Institute for Near East Policy |year=2022 |isbn=979-8-9854474-4-6 |location=Washington, DC |pages=7–12 |chapter=2: The Development of Political Jihadism}}</ref><ref name="newsok" /> It was formed on 28{{nbsp}}January 2017 as a merger of several armed groups: [[Al-Nusra Front#As Jabhat Fatah Al-Sham (July 2016 – January 2017)|Jabhat Fatah al-Sham]] (JFS), [[Ansar al-Din Front]], [[Jaysh al-Sunna]], [[Liwa al-Haqq (Idlib)|Liwa al-Haqq]] and the [[Nour al-Din al-Zenki Movement]].<ref name="new entity" /><ref name="Mona Alami">{{cite web |first=Mona |last=Alami |date=6 December 2017 |title=Syria's Largest Militant Alliance Steps Further Away From Al-Qaeda |url=https://www.newsdeeply.com/syria/articles/2017/12/06/syrias-largest-militant-alliance-steps-further-away-from-al-qaida |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190215114842/https://www.newsdeeply.com/syria/articles/2017/12/06/syrias-largest-militant-alliance-steps-further-away-from-al-qaida |archive-date=15 February 2019 |access-date=19 December 2018 |website=Syria Deply}}</ref> The unification process was held under the initiative of [[Abu Jaber Sheikh]], who had been the second [[emir]] of [[Ahrar al-Sham]]. HTS, along with other [[Syrian opposition]] groups, [[2024 Syrian opposition offensives|launched an offensive]] that led to the [[fall of the Assad regime]] on 8 December 2024, more than 13 years after the start of the [[Syrian revolution]].<ref name="new entity" />

Proclaiming the nascent organisation as "a new stage in the life of the blessed revolution", [[Abu Jaber Sheikh]] urged all factions of the [[Syrian opposition]] to unite under its Islamic leadership and wage a "popular ''[[jihad]]''" to achieve the objectives of the 2011 revolution, which he characterised as the ousting of the [[Ba'athist Syria|Ba'athist regime]] and [[Hezbollah]] militants from [[Syria|Syrian territories]], and the formation of [[Islamic state|an Islamic government]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Joscelyn |first=Thomas |date=10 February 2017 |title=Hay'at Tahrir al Sham leader calls for 'unity' in Syrian insurgency |url=https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2017/02/hayat-tahrir-al-sham-leader-calls-for-unity-in-syrian-insurgency.php |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170211001547/https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2017/02/hayat-tahrir-al-sham-leader-calls-for-unity-in-syrian-insurgency.php |archive-date=11 February 2017 |website=Long Wars Journal}}</ref> After the announcement, additional groups and individuals joined. The merged group has been primarily led by Jabhat Fatah al-Sham and former [[Ahrar al-Sham]] leaders, although the High Command also has representation from other groups.<ref name="Reuters01-28-17">{{cite news|url=https://www.reuters.com/article/us-mideast-crisis-syria-rebels-idUSKBN15C0MV|title=Syria Islamist factions, including former al Qaeda branch, join forces: statement|date=28 January 2017|access-date=16 March 2025|work=[[Reuters]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170128172736/http://www.reuters.com/article/us-mideast-crisis-syria-rebels-idUSKBN15C0MV|archive-date=28 January 2017|url-status=live}}</ref> The Nour al-Din al-Zenki Movement<ref name=defect>{{cite web|url=http://syriadirect.org/news/nour-e-din-a-zinki-defects-from-hts-citing-unwillingness-to-end-rebel-infighting/|title=Nour e-Din a-Zinki defects from HTS, citing unwillingness to end rebel infighting|work=[[Syria Direct]]|date=20 July 2017|access-date=21 July 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170725191422/http://syriadirect.org/news/nour-e-din-a-zinki-defects-from-hts-citing-unwillingness-to-end-rebel-infighting/|archive-date=25 July 2017|url-status=live}}</ref> split from Tahrir al-Sham in July 2017, and the [[Ansar al-Din Front]] in 2018.<ref name="auto9">{{cite web|url=http://nedaa-sy.com/en/news/4175|title=New component split from 'Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham'|work=Syria Call|date=9 February 2018|access-date=10 February 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180213075603/http://www.nedaa-sy.com/en/news/4175|archive-date=13 February 2018|url-status=live}}</ref>

The formation of HTS was followed by a string of assassinations of its supporters. In response, HTS launched a successful crackdown on [[al-Qaeda]] loyalists, which cemented its power in [[Idlib]]. HTS then pursued a "Syrianisation" programme, focused on establishing a stable civilian administration that provides services and connects to humanitarian organizations in addition to maintaining law and order.<ref name="Mona Alami" /> Tahrir al-Sham's strategy was based on expanding its territorial control in Syria, establishing governance and mobilising popular support. In 2017, HTS permitted [[Turkish troops]] to patrol North-West Syria as part of a ceasefire brokered through the [[Astana Platform|Astana negotiations]]. Its policies brought it into conflict with [[Hurras al-Din]], al-Qaeda's Syrian wing, including [[Idlib Governorate clashes (June 2020)|militarily]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Tsurkov |first=Elizabeth |title=Hayat Tahrir al-Sham (Syria) |url=https://ecfr.eu/special/mena-armed-groups/hayat-tahrir-al-sham-syria/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201112020822/https://ecfr.eu/special/mena-armed-groups/hayat-tahrir-al-sham-syria/ |archive-date=12 November 2020 |publisher=European Council on Foreign Relations|date=10 November 2020 }}</ref> HTS had an estimated 6,000–15,000 members in 2022.

From 2017 to 2024, HTS gave allegiance to the [[Syrian Salvation Government]] (SSG), which served as its civilian administration in [[Idlib Governorate]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2017/09/syrian-general-conference-faces-interim-government-idlib/ |title=The Syrian General Conference Faces the Interim Government in Idlib |date=18 September 2017 |publisher=Enab Baladi |access-date=13 December 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171123205055/http://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2017/09/syrian-general-conference-faces-interim-government-idlib/ |archive-date=23 November 2017 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.shaam.org/news/syria-news/%D8%AA%D8%B9%D8%B1%D9%8A%D9%81-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B3%D9%85-%D9%88%D9%83%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%B5%D9%8A%D9%84-%D9%84%D9%80-11-%D9%88%D8%B2%D9%8A%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%8B-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%88%D9%85%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D9%86%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%B0-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AE%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%88%D8%B1%D9%8A.html |title=Syria news |website=Shaam network- www.shaam.org |access-date=18 September 2020 |archive-date=9 October 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181009124057/http://www.shaam.org/news/syria-news/%D8%AA%D8%B9%D8%B1%D9%8A%D9%81-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B3%D9%85-%D9%88%D9%83%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%B5%D9%8A%D9%84-%D9%84%D9%80-11-%D9%88%D8%B2%D9%8A%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%8B-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%88%D9%85%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D9%86%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%B0-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AE%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%88%D8%B1%D9%8A.html |url-status=dead }}</ref> While the organisation officially adhered to the [[Salafi movement|Salafi]] school, the High Council of Fatwa of the Syrian Salvation Government{{snd}}to which it is religiously beholden{{snd}}consisted of ''[[Ulama|ulema]]'' from [[Ash'ari]]te and [[Sufism|Sufi]] traditions as well. ==History==
{{Jihadism sidebar}}

===Predecessors===
Before becoming the emir of al-Nusra Front, [[Ahmed al-Sharaa]] started his military career in 2003, travelling from the Syrian capital Damascus to Iraq, where he resisted the [[U.S. occupation of Iraq]]. He joined [[al-Qaeda in Iraq]] and fought in the [[2003–2006 phase of the Iraqi insurgency|Iraqi insurgency]] against the U.S. occupation.<ref name="Al Jazeera">{{Cite news |date=4 December 2024 |title=Who is Abu Mohammed al-Julani, leader of HTS in Syria? |url=https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/12/4/who-is-abu-mohamad-al-julani-the-leader-of-hayat-tahrir-al-sham-in-syria |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241208141238/https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/12/4/who-is-abu-mohamad-al-julani-the-leader-of-hayat-tahrir-al-sham-in-syria |archive-date=8 December 2024 |work=Al Jazeera}}</ref><ref name="Raya Jalabi">{{cite web|title=Abu Mohammad al-Jolani, the Syrian rebel leader hoping to overthrow Assad|url=https://www.ft.com/content/574cc17a-fa3a-411b-acb0-34fc032c7fe4|website=Financial Times|author=Raya Jalabi|date=7 December 2024|access-date=7 December 2024}}</ref> After his imprisonment by the U.S. military in 2006 and subsequent release by the Iraqi government in 2011, al-Sharaa was tasked with establishing [[al-Nusra Front]] as [[al-Qaeda|al-Qaeda's]] branch in Syria. Between 2011 and 2012, he coordinated with [[Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi]], leader of the [[Islamic State of Iraq]] (ISI), to expand al-Qaeda's mission in Syria.<ref name="Raya Jalabi"/><ref>{{cite magazine | last1=John | first1=Tara | url=https://time.com/4428696/nusra-front-syria-terror-al-qaeda/ | title=Everything You Need to Know About the New Nusra Front | magazine=TIME | date=28 July 2016 }}</ref><ref name="Al Jazeera"/>

In April 2013, Baghdadi announced his group's expansion into Syria and declared the creation of the [[Islamic State]] (ISIL) and unilaterally demanded al-Nusra's merger into ISIL. Al-Sharaa denounced the move; and maintained his ''[[bay'ah]]'' (trans: "pledge of allegiance") to al-Qaeda.<ref name="Al Jazeera"/> al-Qaeda emir [[Ayman al-Zawahiri]] also denounced al-Baghdadi's announcement, asserting that [[Syria]] was the "spatial state" of al-Nusra Front. Zawahiri officially demanded the dissolution of the new entity and urged Baghdadi to withdraw all his troops from Syria.<ref name="Atassi">{{Cite news |last=Atassi |first=Basma |date=9 June 2013 |title=Qaeda chief annuls Syrian-Iraqi jihad merger |url=https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2013/6/9/qaeda-chief-annuls-syrian-iraqi-jihad-merger |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201221100148/https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2013/6/9/qaeda-chief-annuls-syrian-iraqi-jihad-merger |archive-date=21 December 2020 |work=Al Jazeera}}</ref><ref name="Lund2">{{Cite web |last=Lund |first=Aron |date=3 February 2014 |title=A Public Service Announcement From Al-Qaeda |url=https://carnegie-mec.org/diwan/54411 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170729032303/https://carnegie-mec.org/diwan/54411 |archive-date=29 July 2017 |website=Carnegie Middle East Center}}</ref><ref name="Bunzel">{{Cite web |last=Bunzel |first=Cole |date=5 October 2013 |title=al-Baghdadi Triumphant |url=https://www.jihadica.com/the-islamic-state-of-disobedience-al-baghdadis-defiance/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131109154128/https://www.jihadica.com/the-islamic-state-of-disobedience-al-baghdadis-defiance/ |archive-date=9 November 2013 |website=Jihadica}}</ref> In a letter addressed to the leaderships of ISIL and al-Nusra Front in June 2013, Ayman al-Zawahiri directly rebuked al-Baghdadi's moves by recognizing al-Nusra Front as the only official Syrian branch of al-Qaeda and demanded the dissolution of ISIL.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Perkoski |first=Evan |title=Divided, Not Conquered: How Rebels Fracture and Splinters Behave |publisher=Oxford University Press |year=2022 |isbn=9780197627075 |location=New York |pages=168, 169 |chapter=5: Al-Qaeda and the Islamic State}}</ref><ref name="Atassi"/>

By January 2014, hostile rhetoric between ISIL and al-Nusra Front escalated into violent conflict.<ref>{{cite news |date=10 January 2014 |title=Jihadists advance on Syria's Raqqa |url=https://now.mmedia.me/lb/en/nowsyrialatestnews/529580-jihadists-advance-on-syrias-raqqa |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140110195616/https://now.mmedia.me/lb/en/nowsyrialatestnews/529580-jihadists-advance-on-syrias-raqqa |archive-date=10 January 2014 |access-date=10 January 2014 |publisher=NOW}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last=Yacoub Oweis |first=Khaled |date=13 January 2014 |title=Group linked to al-Qaeda regains ground in northeast Syria |url=https://www.reuters.com/article/us-syria-crisis-qaeda-idUSBREA0B0JD20140112 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160203171048/https://www.reuters.com/article/us-syria-crisis-qaeda-idUSBREA0B0JD20140112 |archive-date=3 February 2016 |access-date= |publisher=Reuters}}</ref> Al-Nusra Front formally cut all factional links to ISIL in February 2014 when al-Qaeda's general command issued an official statement disavowing and terminating all relations with ISIL.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Perkoski |first=Evan |title=Divided, Not Conquered: How Rebels Fracture and Splinters Behave |publisher=Oxford University Press |year=2022 |isbn=9780197627075 |location=New York |pages=158 |chapter=5: Al-Qaeda and the Islamic State}}</ref><ref name="Lund2"/><ref>{{Cite news |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/middle_east/al-qaeda-disavows-any-ties-with-radical-islamist-isis-group-in-syria-iraq/2014/02/03/2c9afc3a-8cef-11e3-98ab-fe5228217bd1_story.html |title=Al-Qaeda disavows any ties with radical Islamist ISIS group in Syria, Iraq - the Washington Post |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |access-date=9 December 2024 |archive-date=25 May 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190525165453/https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/middle_east/al-qaeda-disavows-any-ties-with-radical-islamist-isis-group-in-syria-iraq/2014/02/03/2c9afc3a-8cef-11e3-98ab-fe5228217bd1_story.html |url-status=live }}</ref> In July 2016, al-Nusra Front under al-Sharaa split from al-Qaeda, rebranding as [[Al-Nusra Front#As Jabhat Fatah Al-Sham (July 2016 – January 2017)|Jabhat Fatah al-Sham]].<ref>{{cite magazine | last1=John | first1=Tara | url=https://time.com/4428696/nusra-front-syria-terror-al-qaeda/ | title=Everything You Need to Know About the New Nusra Front | magazine=TIME | date=28 July 2016 | access-date=9 December 2024 | archive-date=22 September 2024 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240922204144/https://time.com/4428696/nusra-front-syria-terror-al-qaeda/ | url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite news | url=https://www.bbc.com/news/world-middle-east-36916606 | title=Syrian Nusra Front announces split from al-Qaeda | work=BBC News | date=28 July 2016 | access-date=8 December 2019 | archive-date=30 July 2016 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160730021539/https://www.bbc.com/news/world-middle-east-36916606 | url-status=live }}</ref> Before that time, HTS espoused hardline Islamist views, such as viewing non-Muslims as second-class citizens, but after 2016, it began moderating some of its positions in an effort to gain wider legitimacy.<ref name=":4">{{Cite web |date=18 December 2024 |title=The Runaway Dictator and prospects for resistance to foreign aggression |url=https://mondoweiss.net/2024/12/the-runaway-dictator-and-prospects-for-resistance-to-foreign-aggression/ |access-date=1 April 2025 |website=Mondoweiss |language=en-US}}</ref>

===Background===
{{main|Jabhat Fateh al-Sham}}

{{see also|October 2016 Idlib Governorate clashes|Second Battle of Maarat al-Nu'man}}
Al-Nusra Front co-operated with [[Ahrar al-Sham]] throughout most of 2015 and 2016. Leading Ahrar al-Sham cleric [[Abu Jaber Sheikh]] saw Jabhat al-Nusra's prior affiliation to al-Qaeda as detrimental to their cause. He also sought to unify Islamist rebel factions.<ref name="Petkova 2018 After Sochi">{{cite news | last=Petkova | first=Mariya | title=After the Sochi agreement, HTS is facing internal divisions | website=Al Jazeera | date=25 September 2018 | url=https://www.aljazeera.com/indepth/features/turkey-idlib-180924191442969.html | access-date=18 December 2018 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181217235116/https://www.aljazeera.com/indepth/features/turkey-idlib-180924191442969.html | archive-date=17 December 2018 | url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="new entity"/><ref name="Joscelyn 2016 unite behind">{{cite web | last=Joscelyn | first=Thomas | title=Aleppo-based rebel groups reportedly unite behind Ahrar al Sham's former top leader | website=FDD's Long War Journal | date=20 February 2016 | url=http://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2016/02/zleppo-based-rebel-groups-unite-behind-ahrar-al-sham-former-top-leader.php | access-date=18 December 2018 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180828090713/https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2016/02/zleppo-based-rebel-groups-unite-behind-ahrar-al-sham-former-top-leader.php | archive-date=28 August 2018 | url-status=live }}</ref> He briefly led a more Islamist and less nationalist faction within Ahrar al-Sham, [[Jaysh al-Ahrar]], which supported a merger with Jabhat Fatah al-Sham, before dissolving the group in early 2017. There were merger talks in late 2016, but these broke down. By January 20, 2017, JFS started launching [[Idlib Governorate clashes (January–March 2017)|attacks on rival Islamist groups]] in Idlib, particularly Ahrar al-Sham, before merging with several other allied Islamist groups into "Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham".<ref name="Combating Terrorism Center at West Point 2018 How al-Qa'ida Lost Control">{{cite news | title=How al-Qa'ida Lost Control of its Syrian Affiliate: The Inside Story – Combating Terrorism Center at West Point | author=Charles Lister | website=Combating Terrorism Center at West Point | date=15 February 2018 | url=https://ctc.usma.edu/al-qaida-lost-control-syrian-affiliate-inside-story/ | access-date=19 December 2018 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181201145330/https://ctc.usma.edu/al-qaida-lost-control-syrian-affiliate-inside-story/ | archive-date=1 December 2018 | url-status=live }}</ref>

===Formation===
Upon its founding, HTS was described by [[Abu Jaber Sheikh]], who became the organisation's first [[emir]], as "an independent entity" formed through the unification of previous groups and factions.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Joscelyn |first=Thomas |date=10 February 2017 |title=Hay'at Tahrir al Sham leader calls for 'unity' in Syrian insurgency |url=https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2017/02/hayat-tahrir-al-sham-leader-calls-for-unity-in-syrian-insurgency.php |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170211001547/https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2017/02/hayat-tahrir-al-sham-leader-calls-for-unity-in-syrian-insurgency.php |archive-date=11 February 2017 |website=Long Wars Journal |quote=The new formation was announced on 28 January 2017. He emphasizes that HTS 'is an independent entity and not an extension of previous organizations or factions.' Instead, he claims, 'it is a merger where all factions and titles were dissolved and disintegrated.'}}</ref>

Charles Lister, an analyst specialising in Syria, noted that Ahrar al-Sham lost around 800–1,000 defectors to HTS but gained 6,000–8,000 fighters through the merger of [[Suqor al-Sham]], [[Army of Mujahideen|Jaish al-Mujahideen]], the [[Fastaqim Union]], the western Aleppo units of the [[Levant Front]], and the Idlib-based units of [[Jaysh al-Islam]]. JFS lost several hundred fighters to Ahrar al-Sham but gained 3,000–5,000 fighters from its merger with Harakat Nour al-Din al-Zinki, Liwa al-Haq, Jaish al-Sunna, and Jabhat Ansar al-Din into HTS.<ref name="Combating Terrorism Center at West Point 2018 How al-Qa'ida Lost Control"/> Nour al-Din al-Zenki previously received support from the United States.<ref>{{cite news |url=http://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/terror-list-omission-1.4114621 |title=Why Syria's al-Qaeda affiliate is no longer on Canada's terror list |access-date=15 May 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170706115740/http://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/terror-list-omission-1.4114621 |archive-date=6 July 2017 |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Consolidation of power (January–August 2017)===
====January====
[[File:Hayat Tahrir al-Sham fighters in Mushairfa, northeastern Hama.png|250px|left|thumb|HTS fighters in the village of Mushairfa, northeast of [[Hama]], during the [[Northeastern Hama offensive (2017)|northeastern Hama offensive]] in October 2017.]]

Throughout January 2017, intense fighting broke out between [[al-Qaeda]] loyalists of [[al-Nusra Front]], before the JFS-[[Ahrar al-Sham]] merger to form HTS on 28 January. Soon after the merger, its new emir Abu Jaber Sheikh announced a ceasefire deal aimed at uniting all [[Syrian opposition (2011–2024)|opposition]] militia factions under a central command. The creation of HTS was described as by the [[Henry Jackson Society]] as a reshaping of dynamics since the [[Syrian revolution|2011 Syrian Revolution]], with a potential to shift the balance of power in the Syrian civil war and diminish al-Qaeda's influence in northern Syria.<ref name="reshapes">{{cite web |url= http://henryjacksonsociety.org/2017/02/06/analysis-al-qaeda-reshapes-the-insurgency-in-northern-syria/ |title= Analysis: 'Al-Qaeda Reshapes the Insurgency in Northern Syria' |date= 6 February 2017 |author= Kyle Orton |publisher= [[Henry Jackson Society]] |url-status= dead |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20170210104907/http://henryjacksonsociety.org/2017/02/06/analysis-al-qaeda-reshapes-the-insurgency-in-northern-syria/ |archive-date= 10 February 2017 |df= dmy-all }}</ref> Between 2017 and 2019, HTS launched a series of crackdowns against al-Qaeda loyalists while also targeting [[Islamic State]] (IS) cells through military operations.<ref name=":0">{{cite book |last=Hamming |first=Tore |title=Jihadi Politics: The Global Jihadi Civil War, 2014–2019 |publisher=Hurst publishers |year=2022 |isbn=9781787387027 |location=London |pages=352, 353 |chapter=12: Al-Qaida and the Syrian Jihad |quote=}}</ref>

On 30 January, ''[[Asharq Al-Awsat|Asharq al-Awsat]]'' reported that HTS had about 31,000 fighters, bolstered by the inclusion of various breakaway factions from [[Ahrar al-Sham]].<ref name="surfacing" />

====February====
On 3 February, a US airstrike struck a HTS headquarters in [[Sarmin]], killing 10 members of HTS and 2 members [[Jund al-Aqsa]].<ref>{{cite web |url= https://www.reuters.com/article/us-mideast-crisis-syria-airstrikes-idUSKBN15I1YY |title= Air strikes kill 12 fighters in Syria's Idlib: monitor |work= Reuters |date= 3 February 2017 |access-date= 2 July 2017 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20170922152408/https://www.reuters.com/article/us-mideast-crisis-syria-airstrikes-idUSKBN15I1YY |archive-date= 22 September 2017 |url-status= live }}</ref><ref name="11 terrorists killed" />

Civilians in the rebel regions that HTS controls have resisted it. On 3 February, hundreds of Syrians demonstrated under the slogan "There is no place for [[al-Qaeda]] in Syria" in the towns of [[Atarib]], [[Azaz]], [[Maarat al-Nu'man]] to protest against HTS. ===Leadership passes from Abu Jaber (late 2017)===
From September to November 2017, there were a series of assassinations of HTS leaders, in particular foreign clerics associated with the most hardline elements, such as Abu Talha al-Ordini, Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Mohajer, Abu Sulaiman al-Maghribi, Abu Yahya al-Tunisi, Suraqa al-Maki and Abu Mohammad al-Sharii, as well as some local military leaders, including Abu Elias al-Baniasi, Mustafa al-Zahri, Saied Nasrallah and Hassan Bakour. There was speculation that the assassinations were carried out either by pro-Turkish perpetrators, given the hostility between Turkey and HTS in Idlib, or by supporters{{who|date=November 2021}} of al-Sharaa's attempt to turn the organization away from hardline Salafi-jihadi positions. ===2020–2022===
===Cooperation with Turkey===
HTS successfully defended Idlib from 2019 to 2020 government offensives. During this period, HTS cemented its security partnership with the [[Turkish military operation in Idlib Governorate|Turkish military]] against the [[Assad regime]].<ref name="Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham"/> The opposition and Islamist groups led by HTS suffered heavy losses as a result of the [[Northwestern Syria offensive (December 2019 – March 2020)|Dawn of Idlib 2]] operation launched under the leadership of the Assad regime between December 2019 and March 2020, and many cities and towns from the Idlib Pocket fell under the control of regime forces. During this time, Turkey was protesting the siege of Turkish military observation points deployed according to the [[Syrian peace process|Astana agreement]] by regime forces and wanted Assad to stop his attack on the Idlib region.<ref>{{Cite news|date=9 February 2020|url=https://www.theguardian.com/theobserver/2020/feb/09/syria-turkey-hit-back-assad-idlib-humanitarian-crisis|title=Turkey threatens to hit back as Assad advances in northern Syria|website=The Guardian|access-date=26 December 2024}}</ref> After 34 Turkish soldiers lost their lives in the [[2020 Balyun airstrikes|Balyut attack]] carried out by Assad's troops, Turkey launched [[Operation Spring Shield]]. As a result of the Turkish counterattack, regime forces and their supporters suffered heavy losses.<ref>{{cite news | last=Waters | first=Gregory | title=The Syrian Regime's Combat Losses in Spring 2020, and What Lies Ahead| website=Middle East Institute | date=18 June 2020 | url=https://www.mei.edu/publications/syrian-regimes-combat-losses-spring-2020-and-what-lies-ahead | access-date=25 December 2024 | quote= ''This cease-fire capped off the deadliest week for Syrian regime forces in recent years. ''  }}</ref><ref>{{cite news | title=Turkey shoots down two Syrian warplanes, destroys armor, kills troops as conflict escalates| newspaper=Washington Post| date=1 March 2020 | url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/middle_east/turkey-shoots-down-two-syrian-warplanes-in-growing-conflict/2020/03/01/d0fd189a-5bbe-11ea-ac50-18701e14e06d_story.html | access-date=25 December 2024 | quote= ''Turkey shot down two warplanes and inflicted heavy losses on ground forces in northwestern Syria on Sunday as the two countries edged closer to an all-out war...The situation on the ground was fluid, and the progress of the Turkish-backed offensive was difficult to assess. === 2023 ===
In 2023, it was reported that Western hostilities towards HTS had decreased, but were still marked by mutual rivalry, due to conflict with American interests in the region.<ref name="International Crisis Group">{{Cite web |date=7 March 2023 |title=Containing Transnational Jihadists in Syria's North West |url=https://www.crisisgroup.org/middle-east-north-africa/east-mediterranean-mena/syria/239-containing-transnational-jihadists-syrias |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230307180851/https://www.crisisgroup.org/middle-east-north-africa/east-mediterranean-mena/syria/239-containing-transnational-jihadists-syrias |archive-date=7 March 2023 |publisher=International Crisis Group}}</ref> The U.S. State Department alleged in 2023 that the group obstructed humanitarian efforts in Idlib through licensing and registration protocols as well as through the imposition of taxes and fees on Western NGOs and regulations on aid distribution and beneficiary selection.<ref name=":32">{{Cite web |date=1 March 2024 |title=Syria 2023 Human Rights Report |url=https://www.state.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/528267-SYRIA-2023-HUMAN-RIGHTS-REPORT.pdf |access-date=8 December 2024 |publisher=United States Department of State |archive-date=21 July 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240721131734/https://www.state.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/528267-SYRIA-2023-HUMAN-RIGHTS-REPORT.pdf |url-status=live }}</ref>

In a speech before the revolutionary conference of [[Syrian Salvation Government]] in May 2023, Julani announced intentions to transform the strategic map of the conflict. Stating that Tahrir al-Sham has achieved remarkable military preparation, Julani asserted that "[[Aleppo]] is the gate to [[Damascus]] and it will be under focus for one or two years."<ref>{{Cite news |last=Ali |first=Malin |date=28 May 2023 |title=HTS' leader pledges to change battles' map in Aleppo |work=North Press Agency |url=https://npasyria.com/en/98598/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230528122412/https://npasyria.com/en/98598/ |archive-date=28 May 2023}}</ref> In May 2023, HTS and SDF announced separate proposals to host millions of [[Refugees of the Syrian civil war|Syrian refugees]] stranded across the neighboring countries, following the [[Arab League]]'s reinstatement of the [[Ba'athist Syria|Assad government]].<ref>{{Cite news |last=Iddon |first=Paul |date=31 May 2023 |title=Why Syria's Kurds and Hayat Tahrir al-Sham are offering to host refugees |work=New Arab |url=https://www.newarab.com/analysis/why-syrias-kurds-and-hts-are-offering-host-refugees |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230603002619/https://www.newarab.com/analysis/why-syrias-kurds-and-hts-are-offering-host-refugees |archive-date=3 June 2023}}</ref>

In June 2023, Tahrir al-Sham and the [[Syrian Democratic Forces]] initiated formal diplomatic talks, concluding an agreement to initiate trade of fuel supplies between [[Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria|Rojava]] and [[Idlib Governorate|Idlib]]. The meetings were held against the backdrop of growing tensions between Turkey and SDF, and SDF's intention to deploy HTS as a check on the growing [[Turkish occupation of northern Syria|Turkish influence in northern Syria]]. For their part, HTS proposed joint [[Counterterrorism|counter-terrorism]] efforts alongside SDF. Apart from economic co-operation, the talks also involved negotiations on political arrangements, such as prospects for a joint HTS–SDF civil administration in the event that [[Syrian National Army|SNA]] forces were expelled from Northwest Syria.<ref>{{Cite news |date=12 June 2023 |title=اتصالات بين "تحرير الشام" و"قسد".. اقتصاد وسياسة ثم إدارة مدنية مشتركة |trans-title=Contacts between Tahrir al-Sham and SDF... economy and politics, then a joint civil administration |work=Syria TV |url=https://www.syria.tv/%D8%A7%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%A7%D9%85-%D9%88%D9%82%D8%B3%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%82%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AF-%D9%88%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%A9-%D8%AB%D9%85-%D8%A5%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%AF%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%83%D8%A9 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230612050023/https://www.syria.tv/%D8%A7%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%A7%D9%85-%D9%88%D9%82%D8%B3%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%82%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AF-%D9%88%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%A9-%D8%AB%D9%85-%D8%A5%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%AF%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%83%D8%A9 |archive-date=12 June 2023}}</ref>

=== 2024 ===
[[File:Northwestern Syria offensive (2024).jpg|thumb|right|[[2024 Syrian opposition offensives|Syrian Opposition offensives]] that overthrew Assad's regime in 11 days]]
In November, the HTS launched the [[Northwestern Syria offensive (2024)|Northwestern Aleppo offensive]], which it called Operation ''Deterrence of Aggression'' reportedly capturing 11 towns and villages in western [[Aleppo Governorate]], capturing the eponymous governorate's capital of [[Aleppo]] four days into the offensive.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Al-Khalidi |first=Suleiman |date=27 November 2024 |title=Syrian rebels launch attack against army in Aleppo province |url=https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/syrian-rebels-launch-attack-against-army-aleppo-province-2024-11-27/ |website=Reuters}}</ref><ref name=":2">{{Cite web |title=HTS Takes Over Aleppo While Making Advances Towards Hama (Open access) |url=https://syria-report.com/hts-takes-over-aleppo-while-making-advances-towards-hama/ |access-date=6 December 2024 |website=Syria Report |language=en-US}}</ref> By 4 December, HTS had captured most of Aleppo Governorate and [[Idlib Governorate]] and began to advance on [[Hama]].<ref name=":2" />

According to [[Murhaf Abu Qasra|Abu Hassan al-Hamawi]], commander of HTS's military wing, the offensive resulted from year-long strategic planning and coordination between opposition groups.<ref name="guardian-plot"/> Prior to the operation, HTS had developed enhanced military capabilities, establishing a dedicated drone unit and indigenous weapons production. The group deployed its newly developed "Shahin" (Arabic for Falcon) suicide drone against regime forces for the first time during the offensive. HTS additionally facilitated the creation of a [[Southern Operations Room|unified operations room in southern Syria]], synchronizing military actions with approximately 25 rebel groups to encircle Damascus from multiple fronts.<ref name="guardian-plot"/><ref name="economist-2024">{{cite news|date=13 December 2024 |title=An interview with the military commander of Syria's new masters |newspaper=The Economist |url=https://www.economist.com/middle-east-and-africa/2024/12/13/an-interview-with-the-military-commander-of-syrias-new-masters }}</ref>

On 5 December, HTS fighters had captured the city of Hama after two-days of fighting in neighboring villages. The group said it would advance on [[Homs]] (40&nbsp;km from Hama).<ref>{{Cite web |last1=Al-Khalidi |first1=Suleiman |last2=Azhari |first2=Timour |date=6 December 2024 |title=Syrian rebels capture key city of Hama in fresh blow to Assad |website=[[Reuters]] |url=https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/syrian-rebels-say-they-have-begun-enter-crucial-city-hama-2024-12-05/}}</ref> The next day, it was reported from a rebel commander and pro-rebel media that Tahir al-Sham forces continued to advance on Homs, reportedly reaching 10&nbsp;km–5&nbsp;km north of the city.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Tapper |first=Malaika Kanaaneh |date=6 December 2024 |title=Syrian rebels threaten strategic city of Homs in latest gains |url=https://www.ft.com/content/749e369f-aa4e-443c-9880-a0eaa7b4c59c |access-date=6 December 2024 |work=Financial Times}}</ref>

On 7–8 December, Damascus [[Fall of Damascus (2024)|fell to Syrian opposition forces]], including HTS, the [[Southern Operations Room]], and the US-backed [[Syrian Free Army]],<ref>{{cite web |last1=Gritten |first1=David |title=What just happened in Syria? |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c99x0l1d432o |website=BBC |access-date=8 December 2024 |archive-date=8 December 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241208102858/https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c99x0l1d432o |url-status=live }}</ref> and Assad fled to Russia.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Tarasova |first1=Darya |title=Assad and his family arrive in Moscow and have been granted asylum, Russian state media reports |url=https://www.cnn.com/world/live-news/syria-civil-war-12-08-2024/index.html |website=CNN |date=8 December 2024 |access-date=8 December 2024 |archive-date=8 December 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241208094129/https://www.cnn.com/world/live-news/syria-civil-war-12-08-2024/index.html |url-status=live }}</ref> On 30 December, HTS commander and the ''de facto'' leader of Syria, [[Ahmed al-Sharaa]], announced that the organisation would be dissolved by 4–5 January 2025,<ref>{{Cite web |date=30 December 2024 |title=Syria's interim leader announces the dissolution of Hayat Tahrir al-Sham |url=https://english.aaj.tv/news/330395390/syrias-interim-leader-announces-the-dissolution-of-hayat-tahrir-al-sham-breaking-aaj-news |access-date=30 December 2024 |website=Aaj English TV |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Al-Sharaa: We will announce the dissolution of Hayat Tahrir al-Sham at the national dialogue conference |url=https://www.mtv.com.lb/en/News/Live-Feed/1534445/al-sharaa--we-will-announce-the-dissolution-of-hayat-tahrir-al-sham-at-the-national-dialogue-conference |access-date=30 December 2024 |website=MTV Lebanon |language=en}}</ref> however, this announced deadline of the formal dissolution was delayed until the finalized process took place on 29 January.<ref name=Dissolution/>

==Ideology and governance==
[[Abu Jaber Sheikh]], a prominent scholar and chief of the Shura Council of Tahrir al-Sham, was arrested several times by the [[Assad regime|Ba'athist regime]], which accused him of holding [[Salafi jihadism|Salafi jihadi]] beliefs. He was imprisoned at Sednaya Prison in 2005 and released in 2011 alongside several jihadist prisoners who later formed Salafi rebel groups during the Syrian civil war.<ref name=jaber/> Abu Jaber advocates for "Popular Jihad," a grassroots approach that focuses on winning popular support before establishing [[Islamic governance]], contrasting with IS's "Elite Jihad," which relies on a vanguard to lead from the top down.<ref name="al-Qaeda's grand plan" /> [[Aymenn Jawad Al-Tamimi|Aymenn Jawad al-Tamimi]] of the [[Combating Terrorism Center]] claimed in February 2017 that despite public statements by Tahrir al-Sham's leaders and partisans, the group was essentially [[al-Qaeda]]-aligned.<ref name="formation" />

On 18 June 2019, HTS released a statement offering condolences to [[Egypt]]'s former president [[Muhammad Morsi]] upon his death in Egyptian custody.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://jihadology.net/2019/06/18/new-statement-from-hayat-ta%E1%B8%A5rir-al-sham-condolences-on-the-dismounting-of-dr-mu%E1%B8%A5ammad-mursi/|title=New statement from Hay'at Taḥrīr al-Shām: 'Condolences on the Dismounting of Dr. Muḥammad Mursī'|website=jihadology.net|access-date=21 August 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190627172443/https://jihadology.net/2019/06/18/new-statement-from-hayat-ta%e1%b8%a5rir-al-sham-condolences-on-the-dismounting-of-dr-mu%e1%b8%a5ammad-mursi/|archive-date=27 June 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> In an interview given to the [[Frontline (American TV program)|PBS Frontline]] documentarian [[Martin Smith (documentarian)|Martin Smith]], [[Ahmed al-Sharaa]] stated regarding the religious doctrines and political goals of Tahrir al-Sham:<ref name="Interview-Abu Mohammad al-Jolani">{{Cite interview |date=2 June 2021<!-- publication date (same as modified) from source JSON schema --> |title=Abu Mohammad al-Jolani: Military Leader, Hayat Tahrir al-Sham |author=Abu Mohammad al-Jolani |subject-link=Ahmed al-Sharaa |interviewer-first=Martin |interviewer-last=Smith |interviewer-link=Martin Smith (documentarian) |series=The Jihadist |work=[[PBS Frontline]] |url=https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/frontline/interview/abu-mohammad-al-jolani/ |access-date=16 January 2023 |archive-date=16 January 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230116192501/https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/frontline/interview/abu-mohammad-al-jolani/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

<blockquote>To limit the description of the HTS to only being a [[Salafi movement|Salafi]] or jihadist, I believe, needs a long discussion. And I don't want to comment on that now, because it would take a lot of research and study. We are trying today to talk about Islam in its real concept, the Islam that seeks to spread justice and aspires for building and for progress, and to protect [[Women in Islam|women]] and preserve their rights, and for education as well. So if we agree that there's an Islamic rule in the liberated areas, we say that there are universities, by Allah's grace, full of students, two-thirds being female students. There are more than 450,000 to 500,000 students enrolled in schools. There are fully functioning hospitals in the liberated areas, and there are people working to build towns and pave roads. Others are trying to establish an economic system for people to live securely and peacefully. And there's a judicial system that seeks to give people back their rights and not only to punish the wrongdoers in the way some people would think, when they hear it is an Islamic or a Salafi group.</blockquote>

Being strongly supportive of the four traditional [[Sunni Islam|Sunni]] ''[[madhahib]]'' (schools of jurisprudence), HTS has promoted and enforced the [[Shafi'i school]] in territories under its control, thereby gaining popular support and influence amongst the Shafi'i-majority population of [[Idlib]]. One objective of the promotion and implementation of the ''madh'hab'' system by HTS has been to foster institutionalisation of governance within the framework of traditional [[Islamic jurisprudence]].<ref>{{Cite book |author1=Drevon, Haenni |title=How Global Jihad Relocalises and Where it Leads: The Case of HTS, the Former AQ Franchise in Syria |author2=Jerome, Patrick |publisher=European University Institute |year=2021 |location=San Domenico di Fiesole (FI), Italy: European University Institute |pages=15, 16 |issn=1028-3625}}</ref> Scholar Abu Abdullah al-Shami, the chief of HTS's religious council, has stated that "relying on the schools of jurisprudence" is a pathway towards "getting closer to people". According to Bassam Sihiouni, an influential member of the High Council of the Fatwa: <blockquote>"The schools of jurisprudence are among the safest ways of preserving a correct and inventive intellectual orientation while applying the laws, ethics and morals of [[Islam]]. On the contrary, abandoning these schools would result in a decline of jurisprudence on the basis of just and righteous thinking."<ref>{{Cite book |author1=Drevon, Haenni |title=How Global Jihad Relocalises and Where it Leads: The Case of HTS, the Former AQ Franchise in Syria |author2=Jerome, Patrick |publisher=European University Institute |year=2021 |location=San Domenico di Fiesole (FI), Italy: European University Institute |pages=16 |issn=1028-3625}}</ref></blockquote>

Ines Barnard and Charlie Winter have described HTS as the "most complex and sophisticated Islamist group" in Syria, which integrates mobilisation of popular support and military operations through its [[strategic communication]] networks.<ref>{{Cite book |author1=Barnard, Winter |author2=Ines, Charlie |title=The Handbook of Media and Culture in the Middle East |publisher=Wiley Blackwell |year=2023 |isbn=9781119637066 |editor-last=F. Khalil, Khiabany, Guaaybess, Yesil |editor-first=Joe, Gholam, Tourya, Bilge |location=Hoboken, New Jersey |pages=399, 400 |chapter=32 – Reframing Jihadism: Deciphering the Identity, Politics, and Agenda of Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham in Northwest Syria |lccn=2022037787}}</ref> HTS has also expressed its support for the right of [[Palestinians]] to [[From the river to the sea|reclaim their land]].<ref>{{cite news |title=Syrians' responses to Hamas' attack{{snd}}and subsequent Israeli airstrikes in the Gaza Strip{{snd}}have been varied, splitting along regional, political, and ideological lines |url=https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/syrians-reactions-first-weeks-israel-hamas-war |work=The Washington Institute for Near East Policy |date=24 October 2023 |access-date=8 December 2024 |url-status=live }}</ref>

In 2024, the [[BBC]] reported that the goal of HTS since its break with al-Qaeda has been limited to trying to establish [[Islamic state|Islamic rule]] in Syria rather than a wider [[caliphate]]. The report stated that the messaging of HTS after the [[fall of the Assad regime]] was "one of inclusiveness and a rejection of violence or revenge".<ref>{{Cite web |title=Who are Syrian rebels Hayat Tahrir al-Sham, HTS? |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/ce313jn453zo |access-date=8 December 2024 |website=www.bbc.com |date=8 December 2024 |language=en-GB |archive-date=8 December 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241208055156/https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/ce313jn453zo |url-status=live }}</ref> According to Iranian foreign minister [[Abbas Araghchi]], HTS gave guarantees to protect [[Shia Islam|Shia]] religious sites in Syria ahead of the fall of the Assad regime.<ref>{{Cite news |author1=Fassihi, Nikounazar |author2=Farnaz, Leily |date=8 December 2024 |title=Stunned Iranian Officials Try to Distance Their Country From Assad |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/12/08/world/middleeast/iran-reaction-assad-syria.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241209001203/https://www.nytimes.com/2024/12/08/world/middleeast/iran-reaction-assad-syria.html |archive-date=9 December 2024 |work=The New York Times}}</ref>

=== Governance ===
Despite being a conservative [[Islamism|Islamist]] political organization, HTS was not socially draconian or extremist in its interpretation of Islamic governance. In contrast to organizations like the more hardline [[Taliban]] or [[Islamic State]], HTS did not impose the harsher aspects and interpretations of [[Sharia]] over the populations of the regions it governed. Taxation in HTS-controlled areas was minimized due to high poverty levels and fear of antagonizing locals to its rule. The organization generally avoided interfering in the lives of women. It did not compel the use [[hijab]]s/[[Niqāb|niqabs]] or discourage [[Female education|women's access to education]]. In 2020, the organization allowed the formation of a volunteer [[Islamic religious police|religious police]] but dismantled it within months, stating the concept had no place in a modern state. The organization was quick to respond to pressure from the local population to change course on policy or rhetoric to avoid public backlashes. The generally lenient approach adopted by HTS was the result of years of internal restructuring that left the group with an uncontested leadership that had adopted a pragmatic outlook.{{Sfn|Khalifa|2023|pp=262–263}}

HTS largely refrained from controlling sectors of governance like education and health. After consolidating administrative control in [[Idlib]], the organization allowed the preexisting Education Directorate to continue its operations. The curricula adopted in schools under HTS control are a mix of [[UNICEF]] and Syrian government textbooks (omitting glorification of the [[Ba'ath Party]] and the [[al-Assad family]]).{{Sfn|Khalifa|2023|p=261}} Educational curricula of Tahrir al-Sham teach the [[Shafi'i school]], the predominant ''madhab'' in Syria, and emphasize the importance of the four traditional [[Madhhab|schools of law]] in ensuring Muslim unity. In theology, Tahrir al-Sham officially follows the [[Athari]] school while also allowing [[Ash'ari]]te scholarship in educational institutes and co-operating with [[mosque]]s run by Ash'ari [[imams]]s. As it consolidated power over time, it also reduced its policy of [[Hisbah|moral policing]], arguing that such duties were the role of "relevant ministries of the [[Syrian Salvation Government|SSG]]".<ref>{{Cite book |author1=Drevon, Haenni |author2=Jerome, Patrick |title=How Global Jihad Relocalises and Where it Leads: The Case of HTS, the Former AQ Franchise in Syria. ISSN |publisher=European University Institute |year=2021 |location=San Domenico di Fiesole (FI), Italy |pages=12–20 |chapter=II: The Political Deprogramming of the Radical Emirate |issn=1028-3625}}</ref> Commenting on the changes since 2020, a female Islamist activist campaigning for women's issues stated:<blockquote>Pressure on individual behaviour fell with the creation of HTS. Before, it would have been inconceivable to imagine that women could talk about politics like this. The pressure to wear the full veil (''[[Niqāb|niqab]]'') has also diminished... this situation is new. All the women wore the full veil after the [[Battle of Idlib (2015)|liberation of Idlib]]. The current transformation exasperates religious hardliners, but more pressure cannot be exerted on society, or it might get out of control.<ref>{{Cite book |author1=Drevon, Haenni |author2=Jerome, Patrick |title=How Global Jihad Relocalises and Where it Leads: The Case of HTS, the Former AQ Franchise in Syria. ISSN |publisher=European University Institute |year=2021 |location=San Domenico di Fiesole (FI), Italy |page=16 |chapter=II: The Political Deprogramming of the Radical Emirate |issn=1028-3625}}</ref></blockquote>

HTS also previously advocated for a centralised [[unitary state]] and rejecting [[federalism]] in the process.<ref name="Unitary and centralisation">{{cite web |title=Pathways to Inclusive Governance in Syria |url=https://www.forumfed.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Pathways-to-Inclusive-Governance-in-Syria-Balancing-Unity-and-Diversity.pdf |author1=Sören Keil |author2=Neophytos Loizides |author3=Eva Savelsberg |author4=Rupak Chattopadhyay |publisher=[[Forum of Federations]] |format=PDF |quote=There was general agreement among most of our interviewees that HTS stands for a unitary and centralized political system, in which the political space for other actors is limited. }}</ref>

=== Protection of minorities ===
HTS, composed primarily of Jabhat al-Nusra (JaN), had for years—since its inception—viewed non-Muslims as second-class citizens to be tolerated. However, following a public rebranding after 2016, HTS publicly distanced itself from some of its earlier hardline positions.<ref name=":4"/> For several years, HTS had worked to significantly improve relations with minority groups under its rule, such as the [[Syriac Christianity|Syrian Christian]] and [[Druze]] communities of [[Idlib]], notably returning property stolen during the civil war and permitting Christians living in its territories to reopen their churches.{{Sfn|Khalifa|2023|p=263}}

Following the fall of the Assad regime, HTS military commander [[Murhaf Abu Qasra]] met with [[Alawites|Alawite]] leaders in [[Latakia]] to provide assurances that the minority group would not face retribution, pointing to the group's treatment of Christians in Aleppo as evidence of their commitment to protecting minorities. The group also announced a general amnesty for former regime soldiers, with exceptions for those who committed war crimes or participated in torture.<ref name=economist-2024/>

In June 2022, HTS leader [[Ahmed al-Sharaa]] met with a delegation of Druze leaders and inaugurated a project that facilitated water supply to Druze-majority villages in the [[Harim Mountains|Jabal al-Summaq]]. At the event, al-Sharaa assured the Druze leaders that he was committed to improving economic conditions in the region and expanding public services provided by the [[Syrian Salvation Government]]. Various Syrian observers viewed the event as part of a public outreach programme of HTS with local Druze residents.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Hardan |first=Mohammed |date=16 June 2022 |title=Syrian jihadi leader courts Druze community in Idlib |work=Al-Monitor |url=https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2022/06/syrian-jihadi-leader-courts-druze-community-idlib |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220616163947/https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2022/06/syrian-jihadi-leader-courts-druze-community-idlib |archive-date=16 June 2022}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |date=9 June 2022 |title=بحضور "الجولاني" افتتاح بئر مياه يغذي قرى جبل السماق في ريف إدلب |trans-title=In the presence of al-Joulani, a water well was opened that supplies the villages of Jabal Al-Summaq in the Idlib countryside |work=الدرر الشامية |url=https://eldorar.com/node/175976 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231124155249/https://eldorar.com/node/175976 |archive-date=24 November 2023}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=al-Nofal |first=Walid |date=7 April 2023 |title=Al-Jolani in Jenderes |work=Syria Direct |url=https://syriadirect.org/al-jolani-in-jenderes-is-hts-using-minorities-as-a-way-into-northern-aleppo/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230413225459/https://syriadirect.org/al-jolani-in-jenderes-is-hts-using-minorities-as-a-way-into-northern-aleppo/ |archive-date=13 April 2023}}</ref>

In 2022, Tahrir al-Sham permitted the re-opening of churches in [[Idlib]], enabling the Christian residents to celebrate [[Mass (liturgy)|Mass]]. After a meeting with members of the Christian clergy and civil activists, al-Sharaa announced his policy to protect Christian participation in their religious rites and celebrations. He also promised the restoration of properties unjustly seized from Christian citizens. Al-Qaeda-aligned [[Hurras al-Din]] condemned the move, accusing the Salvation Government of changing Idlib to be "less Muslim". In response, Tahrir al-Sham leaders maintained that ''[[sharia]]'' (Islamic law) safeguarded the rights of non-Muslim citizens (''[[dhimmi]]'' or ''[[musta'min]]'') to observe and teach their religious rites within their communities, arguing for the need for tolerance and peaceful relations between religious communities living in an [[Islamic state|Islamic government]].<ref>{{Cite news |date=8 September 2022 |title=Islamist militiamen in Tahrir al Sham allow Christians to publicly celebrate Mass in the province of Idlib |work=Agenzia Fides |url=http://www.fides.org/en/news/72770-ASIA_SYRIA_Islamist_militiamen_in_Tahrir_al_Sham_allow_Christians_to_publicly_celebrate_Mass_in_the_province_of_Idlib |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220909071007/http://www.fides.org/en/news/72770-ASIA_SYRIA_Islamist_militiamen_in_Tahrir_al_Sham_allow_Christians_to_publicly_celebrate_Mass_in_the_province_of_Idlib |archive-date=9 September 2022}}</ref>

Hanna Celluf, [[Archpriest]] of a 1000-year-old church in [[al-Quniyah]] and a member of the [[Franciscan Order]], stated that Idlib residents have been free to practice their religious rites and expressed his pride in serving the Christians of Idlib.<ref>{{Cite news |author1=Karacaoglu, Karaahmet |author2=Mehmet Burak, Ahmet |date=22 April 2022 |title=Christians in Syria's Idlib worship freely: Archpriest |work=Anadolu Agency |url=https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/christians-in-syrias-idlib-worship-freely-archpriest/2569913 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221015163437/https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/christians-in-syrias-idlib-worship-freely-archpriest/2569913 |archive-date=15 October 2022}}</ref> In December 2024, following the swift overthrow of Assad, the group's spokesman, [[Hassan Abdul Ghani]], stated that all religions will be free in the new Syrian state now that tyranny is gone forever.<ref>{{Cite web |agency=[[Agence France-Presse]] |date=7 December 2024 |title=Successful Syria Rebels Vow no More 'Sectarianism and Tyranny' |url=https://www.kyivpost.com/post/43474 |access-date=8 December 2024 |website=[[Kyiv Post]] |language=en}}</ref>

===Leadership===
Since October 2017 until 29 January 2025, the "general commander" or emir of Tahrir al-Sham was [[Ahmed al-Sharaa]], who was also Tahrir al-Sham's "military commander" and the emir of [[Al-Nusra Front|Jabhat Fatah al-Sham]], who also led its predecessor organisation al-Nusra Front, the Syrian branch of [[al-Qaeda]] until 2016.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-middle-east-36916606|title=Syrian Nusra Front announces split from al-Qaeda|work=BBC News|date=29 July 2016|access-date=30 July 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160730021539/https://www.bbc.com/news/world-middle-east-36916606|archive-date=30 July 2016|url-status=live}}</ref> He would later abandon the use of his nom de guerre and became known by his birth name, Ahmed al-Sharaa.

Previously, the general commander of Tahrir al-Sham was Hashim al-Shaykh, also known as Abu Jaber, who was the leader of [[Ahrar al-Sham]] between September 2014 and September 2015.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.middleeasteye.net/news/aleppo-rebels-united-under-former-ahrar-al-sham-commander-1930344528|title=Aleppo rebels unite under former Ahrar al-Sham commander|work=Middle East Eye|author=Alex MacDonald|date=16 February 2016|access-date=30 January 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170204233653/http://www.middleeasteye.net/news/aleppo-rebels-united-under-former-ahrar-al-sham-commander-1930344528|archive-date=4 February 2017|url-status=live}}</ref> On 1 October 2017, Abu Jaber resigned from his position as the general leader of Tahrir al-Sham and was replaced by Ahmed al-Sharaa. Abu Jaber then assumed the position of head of HTS's Shura council.<ref name=fate/>

Individuals in ''italic'' were defectors from [[Ahrar al-Sham]], which either left to join [[Al-Nusra Front|Jabhat Fatah al-Sham]] in the last few days of its existence, or directly joined Tahrir al-Sham.

==Political relations==
===Al-Qaeda===
====Initial Disputes (2017–2019)====
During its foundation declaration, [[Abu Jaber Sheikh]] had described the Levant Liberation Committee as "an independent entity" free from all the previous relations and allegiances as a result of the newly formed union with various Syrian Islamic militias, thereby disassociating itself from previously dissolved factions such as the al-Nusra Front.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Joscelyn |first=Thomas |date=10 February 2017 |title=Hay'at Tahrir al Sham leader calls for 'unity' in Syrian insurgency |url=https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2017/02/hayat-tahrir-al-sham-leader-calls-for-unity-in-syrian-insurgency.php |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170211001547/https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2017/02/hayat-tahrir-al-sham-leader-calls-for-unity-in-syrian-insurgency.php |archive-date=11 February 2017 |website=Long Wars Journal |quote=He emphasizes that HTS 'is an independent entity and not an extension of previous organizations or factions.' Instead, he claims, 'it is a merger where all factions and titles were dissolved and disintegrated.' With these lines, Abu Jaber undoubtedly intends to distance HTS from the legacy of al Qaeda's official arm, which he and others now argue no longer exits.}}</ref> Since officially disassociating from al-Qaeda in 2017, Tahrir al-Sham has formally established governance over many parts of North-West Syria.<ref name="Ioanes">{{Cite news |last=Ioanes |first=Ellen |date=12 February 2023 |title=Why the earthquake caused a 'perfect storm' of a crisis in Syria |work=Vox |url=https://www.vox.com/world/2023/2/12/23594720/earthquake-aid-crisis-syria-united-nations |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230214211353/https://www.vox.com/world/2023/2/12/23594720/earthquake-aid-crisis-syria-united-nations |archive-date=14 February 2023}}</ref>

In November 2017, HTS launched a wide-scale crackdown on al-Qaeda elements in Idlib and arrested prominent leaders from [[AfPak|Af-Pak region]] and al-Nusra Front such as [[Sami al-Oraydi]]. Emir [[Ayman al-Zawahiri]] opposed the split of HTS from al-Qaeda, stating that it was a treasonous act done without his consent and further denounced the clampdown on foreign Jihadist fighters through an audio-statement.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://aawsat.com/english/home/article/1099286/tahrir-al-sham-arrests-qaeda-leaders-cranks-zawahiri-julani-dispute|title=Tahrir al-Sham Arrests of Qaeda Leaders Cranks up Zawahiri–Julani Dispute|date=30 November 2017|publisher=Asharq Al-Awsat|access-date=13 March 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180302164124/https://aawsat.com/english/home/article/1099286/tahrir-al-sham-arrests-qaeda-leaders-cranks-zawahiri-julani-dispute|archive-date=2 March 2018|url-status=live}}</ref> Several al-Qaeda circles and supporters have also condemned Joulani and compared him to [[Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi]] because of the group's [[October 2016 Idlib Governorate clashes|conflicts]] with other rebel groups, and have described him as an 'opportunist' as well as making claims that he is an agent of foreign powers.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.siteintelgroup.com/blog/index.php/categories/jihad/entry/434-has-al-qaeda-replanted-its-flag-in-syria-1|title=Has al-Qaeda Replanted its Flag in Syria?|website=news.siteintelgroup.com|date=28 March 2018 |access-date=3 April 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180404073504/http://news.siteintelgroup.com/blog/index.php/categories/jihad/entry/434-has-al-qaeda-replanted-its-flag-in-syria-1|archive-date=4 April 2018|url-status=live}}</ref>

====Conflict (since 2020)====
After reaching a ceasefire deal brokered by Turkey, HTS turned its attention to destroying al-Qaeda cells and [[Islamic State]] remnants in [[Idlib]].<ref name="International Crisis Group"/> Rivalry between al-Qaeda aligned [[Hurras al-Din]] and Tahrir al-Sham had begun to escalate violently as early as December 2019. In February 2021, HTS intensified its fight against [[al-Qaeda]] cells by launching a large-scale crackdown that saw many military commanders and leaders of Hurras al-Din incarcerated.<ref name="almcam" /> According to [[Istanbul]]-based academic Abbas Sharifeh, the measures were part of a strategy by HTS for governance consolidation: "Golani simply does not want any competitors in Idlib, especially from a jihadi current affiliated with al-Qaeda."<ref name="almcam" /> By 2023 Tahrir al-Sham had eliminated most of the clandestine networks of al-Qaeda and the Islamic State, disarmed the militias and established total control over Idlib.<ref name="International Crisis Group"/>

In 2019, U.S. government alleged that Tahrir al-Sham was working with [[al-Qaeda]]'s Syrian branch on a covert level, despite its self-identification as a distinct organisation.<ref name=eightyears>{{cite web |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-48056433 |title=Osama bin Laden: Eight years after his death, where is al-Qaeda? |last=Ali |first=Zulfiqar |date=7 May 2019 |access-date=20 May 2019 |website=BBC |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190509103908/https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-48056433 |archive-date=9 May 2019 |url-status=live }}</ref> Some analysts assert that many of the group's senior figures, particularly Abu Jaber, held similarly extreme views.<ref name="al-Qaeda's grand plan">{{cite web|url=http://stopterrorfinance.org/stories/511082466-al-qaeda-s-grand-plan-for-syria-passes-through-hayat-tahrir-al-sham|title=Al-Qaeda's Grand Plan for Syria Passes Through Hayat Tahrir al-Sham|work=CATF |access-date=20 February 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170430221229/http://stopterrorfinance.org/stories/511082466-al-qaeda-s-grand-plan-for-syria-passes-through-hayat-tahrir-al-sham|archive-date=30 April 2017|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref name="bbc">{{cite news|url=https://www.bbc.com/news/world-middle-east-38934206|title=Tahrir al-Sham: Al-Qaeda's latest incarnation in Syria|work=BBC News|date=28 February 2017|access-date=8 January 2025}}</ref> However, Tahrir al-Sham has officially denied being part of al-Qaeda and said in a statement that the group is "an independent entity and not an extension of previous organizations or factions".<ref name=jaber/> In his 2021 interview to ''PBS News'', [[Ahmed al-Sharaa]] argued that financial co-operation with al-Qaeda was necessary to defend Syrians from the tyranny of the [[Assad regime]], and stated that "even at that time when we were with Al Qaeda, we were against external attacks".<ref name="Interview-Abu Mohammad al-Jolani"/> Clarifying the reasons behind the termination of relations with al-Qaeda, al-Sharaa said: <blockquote>"[W]hen we saw that the interest of the revolution and the interest of the [[Syrians|people of Syria]] was also to break up from [the] Al Qaeda organization, we initiated this ourselves without pressure from anybody, without anybody talking to us about it or requesting anything. It was an individual, personal initiative based on what we thought was in the public interest that benefits the [[2011 Syrian Revolution|Syrian revolution]]."<ref name="Interview-Abu Mohammad al-Jolani"/></blockquote>

===Ahrar al-Sham===
According to [[Abdul Razzaq al-Mahdi]], who was a leading scholar in Tahrir al-Sham, the two groups do not particularly hate one another on the political or social battlefield. Certain members, however, believed that a war between the two was possible, since Ahrar al-Sham's attendance at the Astana talks labels it as a "moderate" faction, often seen as blasphemy within groups such as Tahrir al-Sham.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kehJqt_CWfI&app=desktop| archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211114/kehJqt_CWfI| archive-date=14 November 2021 | url-status=live|title=Is Tahreer Sham gearing up for a fight with Ahrar?|author=OGN TV|website=[[YouTube]]|date=2 February 2017}}{{cbignore}}</ref>

In February 2018 [[Ahrar al-Sham]] and the [[Nour al-Din al-Zenki Movement]] merged and formed the [[Syrian Liberation Front]] then launched an [[Idlib Governorate clashes (February 2018)|offensive]] against Tahrir al-Sham seizing several villages and the city of [[Maarrat al-Nu'man]].{{citation needed|date=January 2025}}

==Foreign support==
===Turkey===
In 2018, Turkey designated Tahrir al-Sham as a terrorist organization and al-Nusra Front in 2014. The Turkish government said it was opposed to Tahrir al-Sham (HTS), and had fought with it and declared it a terrorist organisation, and HTS's [[Syrian Salvation Government]] was a direct challenge to the Turkish-backed [[Syrian Interim Government]].<ref name=Cling/>

However, since 2017 there have been times where HTS, particularly its pragmatic faction around [[Ahmed al-Sharaa]], has fought alongside the [[Turkish-backed Free Syrian Army]]. HTS has not stopped Turkey from setting up several [[Turkish military operation in Idlib Governorate|observation posts in Idlib]] and has joined joint operations rooms with Turkish-backed groups while preserving its autonomy. A 2024 peer-reviewed study by political scientist Jérôme Drevon found that HTS maintained security and military coordination with Turkey but retained substantially greater agency than factions of the [[Syrian National Army]], which received direct Turkish support.<ref>{{cite journal |last=Drevon |first=Jérôme |date=2024 |title=Can Jihadis' Strategic Interests Trump Their Ideology? === European Union ===
In February 2025, the [[European Union]] announced the suspension of select sanctions on [[Syria]], including in the energy and banking sectors, as part of an effort to support political transition and economic recovery. The decision was aimed at encouraging HTS to adopt a more inclusive governance approach and uphold human rights. However, the EU maintained key sanctions, particularly those related to [[Arms industry|arms]], and warned that further measures could be imposed if HTS failed to meet its commitments.<ref>{{Cite web |title=EU suspends select Syria sanctions to encourage democratic development |url=https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/2/24/eu-suspends-select-syria-sanctions-to-encourage-democratic-development |access-date=25 February 2025 |website=Al Jazeera |language=en}}</ref> Since then, the European Union has continued easing restrictions on Syria, including removing Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham from its sanctions list on 11 March 2026<ref name=":8" />

=== GCC ===
In April 2025, [[Saudi Arabia]] and [[Qatar]] agreed to settle all of Syria's debts at the [[World Bank]], approximately 15 million US dollars.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Saudi Arabia, Qatar to settle Syria's outstanding debt to World Bank |url=https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/4/27/saudi-arabia-qatar-to-settle-syrias-outstanding-debt-to-world-bank |access-date=2025-06-29 |website=Al Jazeera |language=en}}</ref> The move was done to enable Syria to resume access to the services of the World Bank after being suspended in 2011. The World Bank announced in May 2025 that Syria no longer had outstanding debts to the [[International Development Association]] (IDA), its fund dedicated to low-income countries.<ref>{{Cite web |title=World Bank says Syria eligible for new loans after debts cleared |url=https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2025/5/16/world-bank-says-syria-eligible-for-new-loans-after-debts-cleared |access-date=2025-06-29 |website=Al Jazeera |language=en}}</ref>

== Human rights violations and war crimes ==
Despite HTS's efforts to publicly distance itself from al-Qaeda and present itself as a legitimate governing body through the Syrian Salvation Government, reports from the United Nations, United States, European Union and human rights organizations document its continued involvement in serious human rights violations and war crimes.<ref name=":323">{{Cite web |date=1 March 2024 |title=Syria 2023 Human Rights Report |url=https://www.state.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/528267-SYRIA-2023-HUMAN-RIGHTS-REPORT.pdf |access-date=8 December 2024 |publisher=United States Department of State}}</ref><ref name=":332">{{Cite web |date=1 October 2023 |title=Syria - Country Focus |url=https://euaa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/publications/2023-10/2023_10_COI_Report_Syria_Country_Focus_EN.pdf |access-date=8 December 2023 |publisher=European Union Agency for Asylum}}</ref><ref name=":43">{{Cite web |date=25 November 2023 |title=SNHR's 12th Annual Report on Violations Against Females in Syria |url=https://snhr.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/R231106E.pdf |access-date=9 December 2024 |publisher=Syrian Network for Human Rights}}</ref><ref name=":422">{{Cite web |date=12 August 2024 |title=Report of the Independent International Commission of Inquiry on the Syrian Arab Republic |url=https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/g24/133/77/pdf/g2413377.pdf |access-date=9 December 2024 |publisher=United Nations}}</ref><ref name=":72">{{Cite web |date=1 November 2022 |title=Religious Freedom in Syria Under Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham (HTS) |url=https://www.uscirf.gov/sites/default/files/2022-11/2022%20Factsheet%20-%20HTS-Syria.pdf |access-date=9 December 2024 |publisher=United States Commission on International Religious Freedom}}</ref>

=== Extrajudicial executions, arbitrary arrests, and torture ===
HTS carried out extrajudicial killings, arbitrary arrests, and forced disappearances.<ref name=":323" /><ref name=":34">{{Cite web |date=1 April 2024 |title=Country Guidance: Syria |url=https://euaa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/publications/2024-04/2024_Country_Guidance_Syria_EN.pdf |access-date=9 December 2024 |publisher=European Union Agency for Asylum}}</ref> Political opponents, journalists, activists, and civilians perceived as critical of HTS are unlawfully detained.<ref name=":34" /> Torture and mistreatment were widespread in detention facilities operated by HTS, with at least 22 documented methods of torture being employed by HTS, including physical and psychological abuse.<ref name=":52">{{Cite web |date=1 March 2023 |title=Syria 2022 Human Rights Report |url=https://www.state.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/415610_SYRIA-2022-HUMAN-RIGHTS-REPORT.pdf |access-date=9 December 2024 |publisher=United States Department of State}}</ref> Confessions obtained under torture are admitted as evidence in HTS courts, while detainees have no legal recourse to challenge their imprisonment.<ref name=":323" /><ref name=":52" /><ref name=":422" /> In 2019 a [[Human Rights Watch]] report accused the group of torturing locals who were documenting their abuses in areas they controlled.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Syria: Arrests, Torture by Armed Group |date=28 January 2019 |url=https://www.hrw.org/news/2019/01/28/syria-arrests-torture-armed-group}}</ref>

=== Violence against women and girls ===
HTS enforced strict, religiously motivated dress codes for women and girls, and significantly restricts their freedom of movement and access to education. Women are only allowed to travel when accompanied by a male relative ([[mahram]]), with violations of religious decrees possibly leading to arrest and punishment.<ref name=":323" /><ref name=":52" /> A U.S. State Department report in 2023 asserted that women in HTS-controlled regions of Idlib faced widespread discrimination and violence, including arbitrary detention, sexual abuse in custody, and death sentences for charges such as "adultery" or "blasphemy".<ref name=":323" /> It also alleged that women were also denied the right to file for divorce and are forbidden from wearing makeup in public and living alone.<ref name=":323" /> US government agency [[USCIRF]] alleged in 2022 that the education ministry of the [[Syrian Salvation Government]] gave instructions to block married female students – including underage girls who were forced into [[child marriage]] – from attending public schools and universities.<ref name=":72" />

Women activists, especially those working in humanitarian or media roles, have been particularly targeted.<ref name=":323" /><ref name=":43" /> Many are accused of high treason or other charges as a pretext to pressure them into ceasing activities critical of HTS.<ref name=":43" /> HTS also arrested women with family ties to rival groups or opposition factions as a tactic to extract leverage or force cooperation.<ref name=":43" /> Female detainees were subjected to degrading conditions, including isolation, threats, lack of medical care, and abuse.<ref name=":323" /><ref name=":43" />

=== Allegations of forced conversions and discrimination against religious minorities ===
A 2022 report issued by the [[United States Commission on International Religious Freedom]] (USCIRF) alleged that while HTS publicly claimed to be tolerant and made official overtures towards religious minorities in Idlib like Druze and Christians, it continued to commit widespread violations of religious freedom and human rights, targeting religious minorities and dissenting Sunni Muslims. According to the USCIRF report, the group forcibly converted Druze to Sunni Islam and that properties of Christians and Druze were systematically confiscated in HTS-controlled territories. Arbitrary arrests, torture, and executions under fabricated religious charges are routine in HTS-controlled territories, with detainees often subjected to abuse and denied legal recourse. The USCIRF concludes that HTS promoted an ideologically driven administration that undermines the region's religious diversity.<ref name=":72" />
Since 2020, the Islamist organization has been less repressive toward religious minorities. Some churches are allowed to hold mass again, but they are prohibited from ringing bells or erecting crosses on their buildings.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.france24.com/en/middle-east/20230511-reporters-notebook-idlib-the-last-islamist-rebel-bastion-in-syria|title=Reporter's notebook: Idlib, the last Islamist rebel bastion in Syria |date=11 May 2023 }}</ref>

=== Political repression ===
HTS systematically suppressed dissent through violent repression, arbitrary arrests and severe mistreatment of critics, including journalists, activists, and civilians.<ref name=":323" /><ref name=":34" /><ref name=":422" /> Protests against HTS were rarely tolerated and often met with violent repression.<ref name=":323" /><ref name=":422" /> Journalists faced significant risks, including threats, imprisonment, and physical abuse, forcing many to flee the region.<ref name=":323" /> HTS employed arbitrary detentions and torture to silence perceived political opponents.<ref name=":323" /><ref name=":34" /><ref name=":422" />

=== Obstruction of humanitarian aid ===
According to a report issued by [[US State Department]] in 2023, HTS significantly obstructed humanitarian aid in areas under its control, severely hindering assistance to those in need, particularly [[internally displaced person]]s.<ref name=":323" /> The group imposes arbitrary taxes on humanitarian shipments and interferes with the distribution of aid.<ref name=":323" /> HTS regulates the flow of assistance, exerting control over the selection of beneficiaries and ensuring that aid is diverted for its own benefit.<ref name=":323" />

=== Children as victims and forced participants of war ===
According to reports by the U.S. Department of State, HTS committed severe violations of [[children's rights]], exploiting them in ways that contravene international law. These reports alleged that HTS used children as [[human shield]]s, [[Suicide attack|suicide bombers]], [[Children in the military|child soldiers]], and [[executioner]]s, forcing them into violent roles in the conflict.<ref name=":332" /><ref name=":62">{{Cite web |date=1 January 2024 |title=2023 Trafficking in Persons Report: Syria |url=https://www.state.gov/reports/2023-trafficking-in-persons-report/syria/ |access-date=9 December 2024 |publisher=United States Department of State}}</ref><ref name=":5">{{Cite web |date=2024 |title=2024 Trafficking in Persons Report: Syria |url=https://www.state.gov/reports/2024-trafficking-in-persons-report/syria/ |access-date=10 December 2024 |publisher=United States Department of State}}</ref>
