The '''Syrian Salvation Government''' ('''SSG'''){{efn|{{langx|ar|حكومة الإنقاذ السورية|Ḥukūmat al-ʾInqādh as-Sūriyya}}}} was an unrecognized [[quasi-state]] in [[Syria]], formed on 2 November 2017 by [[Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham]] (HTS) and other [[Syrian opposition to Bashar al-Assad|opposition groups]] during the [[Syrian civil war]], as an administration responsible for managing the affairs of civilians in areas under its control. It controlled parts of northwestern Syria, primarily in the [[Idlib Governorate]] and [[Aleppo Governorate]], as well as smaller areas of the [[Hama Governorate]] and [[Latakia Governorate]].<ref name="Syria Direct 2017">{{cite web | title=HTS-backed civil authority moves against rivals in latest power grab in northwest Syria | website=Syria Direct | date=13 December 2017 | url=https://syriadirect.org/news/hts-backed-civil-authority-moves-against-rivals-in-latest-power-grab-in-northwest-syria/ | access-date=14 November 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180919112904/https://syriadirect.org/news/hts-backed-civil-authority-moves-against-rivals-in-latest-power-grab-in-northwest-syria/|archive-date=19 September 2018}}</ref>

The SSG had an estimated population of over 4,000,000 in 2023 and Its ''de facto'' capital was [[Idlib]].The SSG was governed as an [[Authoritarianism|authoritarian]]<ref name=":22">{{Cite web |last= |first= |date=2023-07-11 |title=Structure of the Syrian Salvation Government |url=https://levant24.com/infographics/2023/07/structure-of-the-syrian-salvation-government/ |access-date=2024-12-04 |website=Levant24 |language=en-US}}</ref><ref name=":16" />{{Rp||page=34}} [[Technocracy|technocratic]]<ref name=":23">{{Cite web |last=Drevon |first=Jerome |date=2020-10-27 |title=The consolidation of a (post-jihadi) technocratic state-let in Idlib |url=https://pomeps.org/the-consolidation-of-a-post-jihadi-technocratic-state-let-in-idlib |access-date=2024-12-03 |website=Project on Middle East Political Science |language=en-US}}</ref><ref name=":24">{{Cite book |last=Zelin |first=Aaron Y. |url=https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/sites/default/files/pdf/PolicyFocus180MargolinZelinIndex.pdf |title=Jihadist Governance and Statecraft |date=10 July 2024 |publisher=[[The Washington Institute for Near East Policy]] |pages=18–19}}</ref><ref name=":21">{{Cite web |last=Zelin |first=Aaron Y. |date=2024-12-03 |title=The Patient Efforts Behind Hayat Tahrir al-Sham's Success in Aleppo |url=https://warontherocks.com/2024/12/the-patient-efforts-behind-hayat-tahrir-al-shams-success-in-aleppo/ |access-date=2024-12-03 |website=War on the Rocks |language=en-US}}</ref> [[Islamic state]] with two branches: the legislative General Shura Council, headed by a president, and the executive branch, headed by a prime minister. Although HTS declared its independence from the SSG, the SSG was widely regarded as its civilian administration that maintained a degree of operational autonomy from the group.<ref name=":16" />{{Rp||page=31}} It has been described as the [[state-building]] project of then-HTS leader [[Ahmed al-Sharaa]].<ref name=":20" /><ref name="auto1">{{cite news |last1=Fahim |first1=Kareem |date=2 January 2022 |title=Former al-Qaeda affiliate in Syria seeks to soften its brand |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2022/01/02/syria-idlib-hts-qaeda-militants/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230324121156/https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2022/01/02/syria-idlib-hts-qaeda-militants/ |archive-date=24 March 2023 |access-date=31 March 2023 |newspaper=[[Washington Post]]}}</ref>

After the December 2024 [[Fall of Damascus (2024)|fall of Damascus]], the final prime minister of [[Ba'athist Syria]], [[Mohammad Ghazi al-Jalali]], transferred power to SSG Prime Minister [[Mohammed al-Bashir]]. Ministers from the Syrian Salvation Government subsequently assumed the same posts in the new [[Syrian caretaker government|caretaker government of Syria]].<ref>{{cite web|accessdate=10 December 2024|date=10 December 2024|url=https://www.independentarabia.com/node/613575/%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%A9/%D9%85%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%A8%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%AA/%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%B4%D9%8A%D8%B1-%D8%B1%D8%B3%D9%85%D9%8A%D8%A7-%D8%B1%D8%A6%D9%8A%D8%B3-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%88%D9%85%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%AA%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%B3%D9%88%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7-%D8%AD%D8%AA%D9%89-%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%B3-2025|website=Independent Arabia|title=Mohammed al-Bashir officially the head of the transitional government in Syria until March 2025}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Syrian President Assad Flees Damascus As Rebels Storm Capital, Take Over Television Network |url=https://www.news18.com/world/syria-rebel-groups-enter-damascus-bashar-al-assad-nowhere-to-be-seen-capture-city-hts-latest-updates-9148928.html |access-date=2024-12-08 |website=News18 |language=en}}</ref>

==Background==
Prior to the formation of the SSG in 2017, [[Idlib Governorate]] was governed by a patchwork of armed opposition groups, [[Cooperative|co-operatively]]-run local councils and independent organizations nominally under the authority of the opposition [[Syrian Interim Government]] (SIG).<ref name=":23" /><ref name="TRTWorld 2017">{{cite web |author=TRTWorld |date=17 November 2017 |title=The shifting red sands of Idlib |url=https://www.trtworld.com/middle-east/the-shifting-red-sands-of-idlib-12370 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190423013852/https://www.trtworld.com/middle-east/the-shifting-red-sands-of-idlib-12370 |archive-date=23 April 2019 |access-date=14 November 2018 |website=The shifting red sands of Idlib}}</ref> As Syrian government offensives encroached on the territory, civil initiatives to create a unified governing body were increasingly undertaken but failed due to a lack of trust and differences of opinion between stakeholders.<ref name=":23" />

HTS and its predecessor organizations, the [[al-Qaeda]] franchise [[al-Nusra Front]] and [[Al-Nusra Front|Jabhat Fatah al-Sham]], have long maintained a presence in Idlib. The al-Nusra Front was not particularly interested in the minutiae of governance, leaving local councils to govern themselves.<ref name="TRTWorld 2017" /> However, in 2013, Ahmed al-Sharaa (Abu Mohammed al-Julani) — then the ''emir'' of al-Nusra — expressed his belief that the popular support required to establish an Islamic emirate could only be obtained through provision of public goods and services, laying the theoretical groundwork for increased involvement in governance.<ref name=":24" />

HTS initially shared its governing responsibilities with other armed groups, but this became increasingly unilateral as al-Sharaa sought to impose his group's hegemony over the province.<ref name=":24" /> HTS engaged in fighting against rival armed groups in the region, such as [[Ahrar al-Sham]], the [[Syrian National Army]] and the local affiliate of the [[Islamic State]], [[Jund al-Aqsa|Liwa al-Aqsa]]. During major offensives in [[Idlib Governorate clashes (January–March 2017)|January–March 2017]] and [[Idlib Governorate clashes (July 2017)|July 2017]], HTS expelled or subjugated these groups, leaving it as the pre-eminent military power in the province.<ref name=":23" />

==History==
The General Syrian Conference, held in Idlib in September 2017, was a continuation of the Civil Administration Initiative in opposition-controlled areas, held at the end of August 2017 in [[Idlib]].<ref name="The Syrian General Conference"/> At its conclusion on 11 September 2017, the Conference formed a constituent body named the General Shura Council, headed by president Bassam al-Sahyouni,<ref name="auto">{{Cite web|date=2020-04-24|title=Salvation Government Elects New Shura Council President|url=https://syrianobserver.com/EN/news/57603/salvation-government-elects-new-shura-council-president.html|access-date=2020-12-09|website=The Syrian Observer|archive-date=2021-04-02|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210402172437/https://syrianobserver.com/news/57603/salvation-government-elects-new-shura-council-president.html|url-status=live}}</ref> and appointed a prime minister. The Syrian Interim Government and the [[Syrian Democratic Forces]] in [[Qamishli]] and [[Afrin Region|Afrin]] rejected the results of the conference.<ref name="The Syrian General Conference">{{cite web |url=http://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2017/09/syrian-general-conference-faces-interim-government-idlib/ |title=The Syrian General Conference Faces the Interim Government in Idlib |date=18 September 2017 |publisher=Enab Baladi |accessdate=13 December 2017 |archive-date=23 November 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171123205055/http://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2017/09/syrian-general-conference-faces-interim-government-idlib/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Conference participants agreed upon "[[Sharia|Islamic law]] as the only source of legislation", "the need to preserve the identity of the Syrian Muslim people", "the overthrow of the illegal regime with all its symbols and pillars and holding it accountable for its committed crimes, as well as liberating the Syrian territory from all the occupying forces, extending security and spreading justice in the liberated areas".<ref name="The Syrian General Conference" />

The move was seen as part of an attempt by HTS to impose its control on the region.<ref name="The Syrian General Conference"/> [[Riad al-Asaad]]'s attendance at the conference was controversial. Riad al-Asaad said that "HTS has previously declared that it will be dissolve itself, which is an external and internal demand", and that HTS "did not attend the conference and we did not communicate with them after it ended, either".<ref name="The Syrian General Conference"/> However, the [[Hawar Kilis Operations Room]], part of the [[Syrian National Army]], condemned Riad al-Asaad and accused him of conspiring with al-Qaeda.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.alalam.ir/news/2010021|title=Euphrates Shield: Riyad al-Assaad is an intruder and conspirator|work=[[Al-Alam News Network]]|date=28 August 2017|access-date=13 November 2018|archive-date=25 August 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180825200608/http://www.alalam.ir/news/2010021|url-status=live}}</ref>

The SSG's Constitution Drafting Assembly appointed Fawaz Hilal as prime minister, alongside nine cabinet ministers, on 10 December 2018. On 23 March 2020, the SSG created an emergency committee to coordinate its response to the [[COVID-19 pandemic in Syria]]. Measures taken by the SSG to prevent the spread of COVID-19 included suspending [[Friday prayer]]s, shutting down schools and markets and opening quarantine centres in [[Jisr al-Shughur District|Jisr al-Shughur]], [[Sarmada]] and Kafr Karmin. However, these efforts were undermined by hardliners from HTS and al-Qaeda's Syrian branch, [[Hurras al-Din]], that continued to pray and hold sermons in mosques without [[social distancing]]. The SSG possessed limited resources to deal with a large outbreak of [[COVID-19]], with only 107 [[ventilator]]s and 243 intensive care unit beds at its disposal.<ref>{{Cite web|title=The Jihadi-Backed Salvation Government and Covid-19 in Northwest Syria|url=https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/view/the-jihadi-backed-salvation-government-and-covid-19-in-northwest-syria|access-date=2020-07-19|website=www.washingtoninstitute.org|language=en|archive-date=2020-05-28|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200528154341/https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/view/the-jihadi-backed-salvation-government-and-covid-19-in-northwest-syria|url-status=live}}</ref>

In March 2024, the Ministry of Interior said it would form a three-judge panel 'security court' for the families of detainees following demonstrations in Idlib due to the death of military personnel in prison and dissatisfaction at HTS influence. In November 2024, HTS-led rebel groups launched the [[2024 Syrian opposition offensives|Syrian opposition offensives]], capturing [[Aleppo]] city and bringing it under the control of the SSG. The SSG reactivated its inter-ministerial emergency response committee to coordinate its expansion into the new territories. The committee prepared tents for new IDPs displaced by bombing, coordinated the transfer of 100,000 loaves of bread from Idlib's bakeries to Aleppo city, and dispatched rubble removal and street cleaning teams.<ref name=":21" /> By December 3, the SSG had opened several government institutions in Aleppo city, began garbage collection, and restarted water and electricity services,<ref name=":26">{{Cite web |last=Zelin |first=Aaron Y. |date=3 December 2024 |title=How Syria's 'Diversity-Friendly' Jihadists Plan on Building a State |url=https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/how-syrias-diversity-friendly-jihadists-plan-building-state |access-date=2024-12-05 |website=[[The Washington Institute for Near East Policy]] |language=en}}</ref> it had also resumed traffic control duties and according to ''[[Reuters]]'', "internet coverage has improved as a rebel-linked telecoms network (Syria Phone) has expanded its reach".<ref name=":19">{{Cite web |last1=Al-Masari |first1=Karam |last2=Gebeily |first2=Maya |date=December 6, 2024 |title=By running Aleppo, Syrian rebels seek to show they are alternative to Assad |url=https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/by-running-aleppo-syrian-rebels-seek-show-they-are-alternative-assad-2024-12-06/ |website=reuters.com}}</ref> HTS Emir al-Julani said that governance duties would be transferred to a "transitional body" in the city rather than the Salvation Government and would withdraw fighters. Dareen Khalifa of the [[International Crisis Group]] in contract with Ahmed al-Sharaa said this was to avoid restrictions on international aid coming into the city due to HTS's terrorist designation.<ref name=":19" />

During the offensive, drones dropped SSG leaflets on government positions which exhorted troops to desert or defect and contained contact details for the SSG's "Center for Safety and Defection".<ref name=":21" /> The SSG offered amnesty to all surrendering government forces who remained in Aleppo after its capture.<ref name=":26" />

==Government and politics==
The SSG was described as centralised,<ref>{{cite web |title=Decentralization in post-Assad Syria: a façade or bottom-up local governance? |url=https://pomeps.org/decentralization-in-post-assad-syria-a-facade-or-bottom-up-local-governance |author=Tiina Hyyppä |publisher=Project on Middle East Political Science |quote=Subsequently, revolutionary activists who could not work under HTS had to flee elsewhere, or out of the country, and power in the opposition-held area, at the time known as the Idlib enclave, home to about four million people, was again centralized under the HTS-affiliated Salvation Government&nbsp;... Some of my interlocutors likened the centralized structure to Assad’s rule. }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Governing the day after in Syria |url=https://mei.edu/publication/governing-day-after-syria/ |publisher=[[Middle East Institute]] |date=2024-12-19 |author=Munqeth Othman Agha |quote=The establishment of the SSG marked a turning point for HTS’s state-building ambitions in Idlib Governorate, becoming the cornerstone of the group’s centralized technocratic governance model. }}</ref> [[Technocracy|technocratic]], and non-ideological in nature.<ref name=":23" /> Many of its positions were occupied by members of the educated urban elite attempting to regain influence after the end of direct governance by armed opposition groups, particularly businessmen and conservative revolutionary activists.<ref name=":23" />

The General Shura Council was the legislative body of the SSG. It was responsible for electing a prime minister and approving ministerial appointments, drafting laws and presenting them for implementation to the executive branch, and ratifying the executive's plans.<ref name=":6" /> The council was also in charge of forming specialized committees that oversaw and scrutinized the work of the executive.<ref name=":6" /><ref name=":15">{{Cite web |last= |first= |date=2024-04-22 |title=Al-Jolani: The one-man rule |url=https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2024/04/al-jolani-the-one-man-rule/ |access-date=2024-12-03 |website=Enab Baladi |language=en-US}}</ref>

The council was composed of representatives of the various "segments" of society,<ref name=":6" /> each having 15 elected members. These segments included trade unions, tribes, internally displaced people and local residents.<ref name=":15" /> Candidates for Council elections were pre-selected and women were not permitted to vote.<ref name=":16">{{Cite book |last=Zelin |first=Aaron Y. |url=https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/sites/default/files/pdf/PolicyFocus175Zelinv2.pdf |title=The Age of Political Jihadism: A Study of Hayat Tahrir al-Sham |date=9 May 2022 |publisher=[[The Washington Institute for Near East Policy]] |isbn=9798985447446 |pages=}}</ref>{{Rp||page=34}} In April 2024, the council announced the selection of an eight-member Higher Election Committee{{Efn|Consists of Essam Al-Khalif (Chairman), Ali Sultan (Deputy), Mohamed Ragheb, Mohamed Mahmoud Al-Zein, Abdul Ghani Sahari, Abdul Nasser Al-Youssef, and Abdul Qader Ghazal.}} to determine a new electoral process and delineate electoral districts for future elections.<ref name=":15" /><ref>{{Cite web |date=March 26, 2024 |title=تمهيداً لاختيار مجلس جديد.. "شورى إدلب" يعيد تشكيل اللجنة العليا للانتخابات |url=https://www.syria.tv/%D8%AA%D9%85%D9%87%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A7%D9%8B-%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AE%D8%AA%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%85%D8%AC%D9%84%D8%B3-%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF-%D8%B4%D9%88%D8%B1%D9%89-%D8%A5%D8%AF%D9%84%D8%A8-%D9%8A%D8%B9%D9%8A%D8%AF-%D8%AA%D8%B4%D9%83%D9%8A%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%86%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A7-%D9%84%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%AA%D8%AE%D8%A7%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%AA |website=syria.tv}}</ref> The committee was reportedly considering giving representation to women, local councils, minorities, and the military.<ref name=":15" />

Women held few positions in the SSG's government and were confined to the Women's Office. As of July 2024, female leadership was absent from 10 of the SSG's 11 ministries, and no woman had ever been granted a ministerial portfolio.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Nelson |first=Mateo |date=2024-07-01 |title='A long way to go': Women left out of leadership in northwestern Syria |url=https://syriadirect.org/women-left-out-of-leadership-in-northwestern-syria/ |access-date=2024-12-03 |website=Syria Direct |language=en-US}}</ref>

The prime minister was elected by the Shura Council. Candidates for prime minister were nominated by council members. A minimum of 10 members had to support a nomination for it to advance to the voting stage. In the event only one candidate advanced to the voting stage, they had to obtain two-thirds of Council's vote to be elected; otherwise, the candidate with the most votes won.<ref name=":15" /> Once elected, the prime minister was required to present his cabinet to the council for approval within 30 days. Prime ministers were elected for one year terms: the first term was a "trial year", after which the Council could elect them for second and third terms.<ref name=":15" />

Prospective prime ministerial candidates had to be Syrian, hold a university degree, be married to a Syrian, not have been convicted of a crime, have a "good reputation" and possess a "revolutionary history".<ref name=":15" />

==== Ministries ====
The SSG consisted of eleven ministries,<ref name=":15" /> under each of which numerous departments and directorates operated.<ref name=":22" /> The SSG originally comprised ten ministries, with the eleventh, the Ministry of Information, being formed in 2023.<ref>{{Cite web |last=S |first=T. |date=2023-12-11 |title=Idleb's Ministry of Information Has All the Powers of a Security Branch - The Syrian Observer |url=https://syrianobserver.com/syrian-actors/idlebs-ministry-of-information-has-all-the-powers-of-a-security-branch.html |access-date=2024-12-04 |language=en-US}}</ref> Five institutions operated independently of a ministry and reported directly to the prime minister.<ref name=":22" />

Ministerial nominees were evaluated based on several criteria, including age and academic qualifications. The nomination process involved discussions with the Shura Council's committees, elites and relevant community and professional bodies.<ref name=":15" />

=== Administrative divisions ===
The SSG's local [[municipal council]]s were organized as the Administration of the Liberated Areas (ALA).<ref name=":21" /> The ALA was administratively divided into eight regions: Central, Northern, [[Sarmada]], [[Harem, Syria|Harem]], [[Jisr ash-Shughur]], [[Ariha, Idlib|Ariha]], [[Atme]], and Idlib.<ref name=":15" /><ref name=":22"/>

=== Foreign relations ===
While no country recognized the SSG as a sovereign state or a legitimate government of Syria, its Department of Political Affairs (DPA) conducted diplomatic outreach. On 29 November 2024, the DPA issued a statement that called on [[Russia]] to end its support for the Syrian government and stated that the DPA sought to "build positive relations based on mutual respect and common interests with all countries of the world, including Russia". It issued a similar statement directed to [[Iraq]] amid rumours that [[Iraqi militias]] were planning to enter Syria to fight alongside government forces.<ref name=":21" />

== Economy ==
The territories controlled by the SSG in Idlib Governorate were primarily rural. The poor level of infrastructure in the region was further degraded by [[Syrian civil war|the civil war]], which destroyed oil production facilities, power plants, and agricultural silos.<ref name=":7">{{Cite web |last=Dagres |first=Holly |date=2024-06-04 |title=Economic recovery in opposition-held Syria is challenging but still possible |url=https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/menasource/economic-recovery-syria-rebuilding-northwest/ |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=Atlantic Council |language=en-US}}</ref>

In a 2022 speech, Ahmed al-Sharaa stated his desire to see the SSG's "rate of exports [become] greater than [its] rate of imports" and to encourage industrial development by simplifying planning laws.<ref name=":20">{{Cite web |last=Zelin |first=Aaron |date=29 August 2022 |title=Jawlani's "State of the Union" |url=https://www.jihadica.com/jawlanis-state-of-the-union/ |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=Jihadica |language=en-US}}</ref> Through its Local Product Protection Department, the SSG's Ministry of Industry has adopted a [[Protectionism|protectionist]] policy of imposing [[tariffs]] on imports that compete with domestically produced goods. However, tariffs on imported raw materials and high energy costs mean that domestic production is unable to compete with imports in practice.<ref name=":14">{{Cite web |last=Ula |first=enab10 |date=2024-10-23 |title=Idlib's industry faces difficulties in production and export |url=https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2024/10/idlibs-industry-faces-difficulties-in-production-and-export/ |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=Enab Baladi |language=en-US}}</ref> Weak domestic demand has also limited industrial development, although some pharmaceutical factories have been established.<ref name=":14" />

Rapid depreciation of the [[Syrian pound]] triggered by the US Government's [[Caesar Syria Civilian Protection Act]] prompted the SSG to replace it with the [[Turkish lira]] in its administered territories in June 2020.<ref name=":17">{{Cite web|date=2020-07-01|title=Residents of northwestern Syria replace Syrian pound with Turkish lira|url=https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2020/07/residents-of-northwestern-syria-replace-syrian-pound-with-turkish-lira/|access-date=2020-07-19|website=Enab Baladi|language=en-US|archive-date=2020-07-05|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200705114336/https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2020/07/residents-of-northwestern-syria-replace-syrian-pound-with-turkish-lira/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Rapid depreciation of the [[Syrian pound]] triggered by the US Government's [[Caesar Syria Civilian Protection Act]] prompted the SSG to replace it with the [[Turkish lira]] in its administered territories in June 2020.<ref name=":17" /> Usage of the Syrian pound was criminalized.<ref name=":8">{{Cite web |title=The Economics of Hayat Tahrir al-Sham |url=https://www.mei.edu/publications/economics-hayat-tahrir-al-sham |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=Middle East Institute |language=en}}</ref> The [[Turkish economic crisis (2018–current)|Turkish lira crisis]] raised the cost of imports, leading to increased unemployment, inflation and commodity prices,<ref>{{Cite news |last=al-Mahmoud |first=Hussam |date=16 March 2022 |title=Tahrir al-Sham's bank in Idlib a need or luxury? |url=https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2022/03/tahrir-al-shams-bank-in-idlib-a-need-or-luxury/ |access-date=2 December 2024 |work=Enab Baladi}}</ref> and severely affecting economic activity.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Rateb |title=Indicators of Early Economic Recovery in NW Syria in 2023 |url=https://omranstudies.org/index.php/publications/reports/indicators-of-early-economic-recovery-in-nw-syria-in-2023.html |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=omranstudies.org |language=en-gb}}</ref> The SSG operates a "General Monetary Agency for Cash Management and Consumer Protection" which regulates foreign exchange rates, ''[[hawala]]'' services and currency exchange firms.<ref name=":8" /> It also operates Sham Bank, a financial institution that is the primary supplier of the lira in its territories.<ref name=":8" />

Sanctions prevent the population of the SSG from accessing international banking services.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Oudeh |first=Elise Daniaud |date=2023-02-20 |title=So Close Yet So Far: The Political and Economic Isolation of Northwest Syria |url=https://timep.org/2023/02/20/so-close-yet-so-far-the-political-and-economic-isolation-of-northwest-syria/ |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=The Tahrir Institute for Middle East Policy |language=en-US}}</ref> Access to finance is generally limited to international grants, investments made by opposition groups, and [[remittance]]s from the [[Syrian diaspora]].<ref name=":7" /> To circumvent these restrictions, both opposition groups and civilians have turned to exchanging [[cryptocurrencies]] such as [[Bitcoin]] and [[Tether (cryptocurrency)|Tether]]. Cryptocurrency stores operate in Idlib and [[Sarmada]]. HTS has encouraged their usage, declaring cryptocurrencies as ''shariah''-compliant and describing Bitcoin as the "Currency of the Future Economy".<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=O'Leary |first=Rachel-Rose |title=The bitcoin terrorists of Idlib are learning new tricks |url=https://www.wired.com/story/bitcoin-crypto-terrorism-syria/ |access-date=2024-12-02 |magazine=Wired |language=en-US |issn=1059-1028}}</ref>

The SSG's General Authority of Zakat managed the collection of ''[[zakat]]'', the Islamic alms-tax, from Muslims living under its administration. Failing to pay the ''zakat'' was illegal and can lead to arrest and imprisonment.<ref>{{Cite web |last=communication |date=2023-10-24 |title=Syria/Idlib: Arbitrary Arrest to Everyone who Refuses to Pay Royalties Under the Pretext of "Collecting Zakat Money" |url=https://stj-sy.org/en/syria-idlib-arbitrary-arrest-to-everyone-who-refuses-to-pay-royalties-under-the-pretext-of-collecting-zakat-money/ |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=Syrians for Truth and Justice |language=en-US}}</ref>

=== Agriculture ===
Agriculture in the SSG's territories was not mechanized. Olive harvesting is the main source of employment for day laborers in Idlib Governorate, and in 2020 agriculture was the primary income source for 36% of households.<ref name=":13">{{Cite web |date=2024-01-25 |title=Although beset with challenges, cotton growing has returned to Idlib |url=https://www.equaltimes.org/although-beset-with-challenges?lang=en |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=Equal Times |language=en}}</ref> Both [[drought]] and [[Overdrafting|groundwater depletion]], exacerbated by illegal drilling and an influx of internally displaced persons into the region, had led to increasing production costs.<ref name=":13" />

The SSG's Ministry of Agriculture managed agricultural affairs and encouraged the cultivation of strategic crops, such as wheat and potatoes.<ref name=":9">{{Cite news |last=Hamza |first=Mahmoud |date=28 May 2024 |title=Unlikely crops find fertile ground in Idlib |url=https://syriadirect.org/unlikely-crops-find-fertile-ground-in-idlib/ |access-date=2 December 2024 |work=Syria Direct}}</ref> It maintained [[seed testing]] laboratories and produced seeds that are distributed to farmers.<ref name=":13" /> In 2023, the ministry produced and distributed 4,600 kilograms of [[cottonseed]] to farmers in an attempt to restart [[cotton]] cultivation in the region, which fell into decline after the reduction of government [[subsidies]] in 2007, and increased female employment.<ref name=":13" />

The Ministry of Economy imposed [[price controls]] on wheat.<ref>{{Cite web |last= |first= |date=2024-05-28 |title=Salvation Govt sets wheat prices in its areas of control |url=https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2024/05/salvation-govt-sets-wheat-prices-in-its-areas-of-control/ |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=Enab Baladi |language=en-US}}</ref> In search of higher profits, farmers increasingly planted crops not traditionally cultivated in the region, including saffron, strawberries, bananas, broccoli and [[Damascus rose|Damask roses]]. These enterprises remained small and experimental though.<ref name=":9" />

=== Energy ===
Most electricity in the SSG's territories was supplied from Turkey by the Green Energy Company,<ref name=":11" /> the local [[Franchising|franchise]] of a Turkish firm of the same name.<ref>{{Cite web |last=sh |first=obaida |date=2021-05-09 |title=Idlib's Electricity Shortage: Finally Resolved? |url=https://levant24.com/articles/2021/05/idlibs-electricity-shortage-finally-resolved/ |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=levant24 |language=en-US}}</ref> Electricity distribution was managed by the SSG's General Electricity Corporation. In May 2021, the Green Energy Company completed the construction of [[Substation|electrical substations]] capable of receiving 66kV from Turkey.<ref name=":11">{{Cite news |date=7 July 2021 |title=Turkish Electricity Company Expands Rapidly in Northwest Syria |url=https://english.aawsat.com/home/article/3067306/turkish-electricity-company-expands-rapidly-northwest-syria |access-date=2 December 2024 |work=[[Asharq Al-Awsat]]}}</ref> Transmission lines connect the substations in [[Reyhanlı]] in Turkey and [[Harem, Syria|Harem]] in Idlib.<ref name=":10">{{Cite web |last=sh |first=obaida |date=2023-05-23 |title=Return of Electricity: Hope for the People of Idlib |url=https://levant24.com/articles/2023/05/return-of-electricity-hope-for-the-people-of-idlib/ |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=levant24 |language=en-US}}</ref> By May 2023, over 70% of areas under the SSG's administration were connected to the electrical grid. Areas not connected to the grid relied on diesel generators and solar panels.<ref name=":10" /> The Green Energy Company maintained an effective [[Natural monopoly|monopoly]] on the supply of electricity and was accused by the [[Syrian Observatory for Human Rights]] of unduly raising prices.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-08-04 |title=To earn more profits {{!}} Electricity company of "HTS" raises its prices and residents in Idlib face stifling living crisis – The Syrian Observatory For Human Rights |url=https://www.syriahr.com/en/340534/?__cf_chl_tk=CRaxGfHv_gFeoXsT8xU86p_N.bUn0blEMG.Hd7h6c0o-1733144025-1.0.1.1-48GBhplbbPaHOgA1Pw.Q6Gnhg9AB2rVlT5CC1DAI85Q |access-date=2024-12-02 |language=en-CA}}</ref>

=== Communication ===
In July 2023, the SSG launched "Syria Phone", the first communication and internet services company in Idlib. The state-owned company announced that it would provide "cellular calls, [[SMS]], [[4G]] and video calls."<ref>{{Cite news |last=al-Sheikh |first=Mo'ayed |date=9 July 2023 |title=HTS Salvation Government launches communication company in Idlib |work=North Press Agency |url=https://npasyria.com/en/100841/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230711151802/https://npasyria.com/en/100841/ |archive-date=11 July 2023}}</ref>

== Military and law enforcement ==
While the SSG did not have a defense ministry as HTS effectively functioned as it. Despite this, the SSG opened a [[Military academy|military college]] in December 2021, whose purpose was described by Ahmed al-Sharaa as to "[increase] the experience of the ''[[Mujahideen|mujahidin]]'' in [[military science]] and martial arts."<ref name=":21" /> Over 400 officers graduated from its first course in 2022.<ref>{{Cite web |last= |date=2022-09-06 |title=In Presence of Julani: Graduation of First Batch from Military College in Idleb |url=https://syrianobserver.com/syrian-actors/in-presence-of-julani-graduation-of-first-batch-from-military-college-in-idleb.html |access-date=2024-12-03 |website=The Syrian Observer |language=en-US}}</ref>

The SSG operated a police force under the Ministry of Interior, the [[General Security Service (Syria)|General Security Service]]. A [[police academy]] was opened in September 2023 and produced its first batch of graduates in August 2024.<ref>{{Cite web |last= |first= |date=2023-09-30 |title=SSG Ministry of Interior Opens First Police College in Greater Idlib |url=https://levant24.com/news/2023/09/ssg-ministry-of-interior-opens-first-police-college-in-greater-idlib/ |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=Levant24 |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |date=15 August 2024 |title=Police Academy Graduation Marks New Milestone in Northern Syria's Development |url=https://levant24.com/news/2024/08/police-academy-graduation-marks-new-milestone-in-northern-syrias-development/ |access-date=2 December 2024 |work=Levant24}}</ref>

In 2024, the interior ministry absorbed two security agencies of HTS after a prisoner abuse scandal led to a protest movement,<ref>{{Cite web |title=HTS Faces an Internal Crisis and Anger on the Streets |url=https://www.jusoor.co/public/en/details/hts-faces-an-internal-crisis-and-anger-on-the-streets |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=Jusoor For Studies |language=en}}</ref> and Ahmed al-Sharaa admitted that HTS had used torture to extract false confessions from prisoners.<ref>{{Cite web |last= |first= |date=2024-02-01 |title=Video Release of Meeting Regarding New Development About Spy Case Within HTS |url=https://levant24.com/news/2024/02/video-released/ |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=Levant24 |language=en-US}}</ref> HTS's General Security Service was transferred to the interior ministry in April 2024,<ref>{{Cite web |last= |first= |date=2024-04-07 |title=Reforms in Idlib: Charting a Path Forward |url=https://levant24.com/news/2024/04/reforms-in-idlib-charting-a-path-forward/ |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=Levant24 |language=en-US}}</ref> followed by its Public Security Service in June.<ref>{{Cite web |last=sh |first=obaida |date=2024-06-02 |title=HTS Responds to US Statement Amidst Ongoing Protests and Calls for Reform |url=https://levant24.com/news/2024/06/hts-responds-to-us-statement-amidst-ongoing-protests-and-calls-for-reform/ |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=levant24 |language=en-US}}</ref>

== Religion ==
HTS had faced internal opposition from hardline [[Salafi jihadism|Salafi jihadis]], who view its moderate outlook as "un-Islamic".<ref name=":23" /> The SSG had institutionalized religious structures and had reasserted the role of the [[Shafi'i school|Shafi'i]] ''[[madhhab]]'' of [[Islamic jurisprudence]], the predominant legal school followed in Idlib, in an effort to marginalize Salafi-jihadi hardliners.<ref name=":27">{{Cite web |last=Wehrey |first=Frederic |date=7 June 2021 |title=Islamic Institutions in Arab States: Mapping the Dynamics of Control, Co-option, and Contention |url=https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2021/06/islamic-institutions-in-arab-states-mapping-the-dynamics-of-control-co-option-and-contention?lang=en |access-date=2024-12-05 |website=Carnegie Endowment for International Peace |language=en}}</ref><ref name=":23" /><ref name=":16" />{{Rp|9}} Idlibi clerics characterized the SSG's policies concerning [[mosque]]s as being "highly centralized and authoritarian".<ref name=":27" /> The Ministry of Awqaf had the power to appoint and dismiss clerics; for example, in 2020, clerics affiliated with al-Qaeda affiliate [[Hurras al-Din]] in the village of Arab Sa'id were replaced by loyalists. "Mosque officers" appointed by the Ministry of Awqaf monitored mosque activities. Independent [[Khatib|''khatibs'']] could be substituted for SSG-loyal preachers during politically sensitive events, such as bouts of rebel infighting. Those who refuse substitution risk dismissal.<ref name=":27" />

Although HTS was [[Salafi]] in ''[[aqidah]]'', the SSG had refrained from imposing a particular strand of Sunni Islam on the populace and predominately [[Sufism|Sufi]] local ''[[ulama]]''.<ref name=":27" /><ref name=":23" /> It allowed politically neutral traditional religious institutes which taught the competing [[Ash'arism|Ash'ari]] school of ''aqidah'' to operate,<ref name=":23" /> including those ran by the [[Muslim Brotherhood in Syria|Muslim Brotherhood]] and [[Tablighi Jamaat]].<ref name=":27" /> It had gradually accommodated a larger diversity of religious views among preachers, an evolution driven partly by a lack of manpower.<ref name=":23" />

Public opposition halted early attempts at imposing religious laws such as women being required to travel with a ''[[mahram]]''.<ref name=":23" /> Schools were [[Gender segregated education|segregated by gender]], but mixing between genders at restaurants and shopping centers was not prevented.<ref name=":25">{{Cite web |last=Uddin |first=Rayhan |date=4 December 2024 |title=What does Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham believe in? |url=https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/what-does-hayat-tahrir-al-sham-believe |access-date=2024-12-05 |website=Middle East Eye |language=en}}</ref> The SSG encouraged women to "dress modestly" and wear the ''[[hijab]]'',<ref name=":25" /><ref name=":16" />{{Rp|38–39}} but did not force them to wear the ''[[niqāb]]''.<ref name=":25" /> The ''sharia''<nowiki/>'s ''[[hudud]]'' punishments, such as stoning and flogging, were not imposed.<ref name=":25" /><ref name=":23" /> In 2020, Ahmed al-Sharaa told HTS fighters that "some people limit the issue of implementing the rule of the ''sharia'' to just imposing some of the hudud punishments, chopping off hands, stoning whomever, whipping someone who drinks alcohol [...] but this is a very basic part of the very big concept of implementing the rule of the ''sharia''".<ref name=":16" />{{Rp|27}}

In December 2024, the SSG's Department for Political Affairs issued a communique addressed to Syria's [[Alawites]], stating that the Assad government had exploited the sect for its survival and had consequently caused "deep societal wounds". It further stated that the "Syrian revolution is a call for freedom, dignity and justice for all under the roof of Syria", and that while it may be "challenging", "wise individuals" from the Alawite sect could lead it to a "just and inclusive future".<ref>{{Cite web |last= |date=2024-12-04 |title=Messages of Reconciliation and Renewal: The Salvation Government and the Future of Syria |url=https://syrianobserver.com/syrian-actors/messages-of-reconciliation-and-renewal-the-salvation-government-and-the-future-of-syria.html |access-date=2024-12-05 |website=The Syrian Observer |language=en-US}}</ref>

== Education ==
The SSG's Ministry of Education supervised the formal schooling system in Idlib, with more than 550,000 students, 1,800 schools and 12 [[universities]] in 2022. These included approximately 950 schools directly operated by the ministry and employed nearly 12,500 staff members, in addition to the private education system authorized by the SSG. The curriculum was a continuation of the pre-2011 Syrian syllabus developed in partnership with [[UNICEF]], with the exception of texts related to the [[Assad regime]] or deemed as contradicting the [[Sharia]] as per the education ministry guidelines. Educational institutions followed [[Sex segregation|gender segregation]] norms.<ref name="auto1"/><ref>{{Cite web |last=Almustafa |first=Aamer |date=9 June 2023 |title=Education System in Northwestern Syria: A Long Road Ahead |url=https://timep.org/2023/06/09/education-system-in-northwestern-syria-a-long-road-ahead/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230609171324/https://timep.org/2023/06/09/education-system-in-northwestern-syria-a-long-road-ahead/ |archive-date=9 June 2023 |website=The Tahrir Institute for Middle East Policy}}</ref>

Education had been severely disrupted by airstrikes on educational facilities and a lack of funding.<ref name=":12">{{Cite web |date=2024-09-18 |title=Attempts to Revive Education Sector in Northern Syria – The Syrian Observer |url=https://syrianobserver.com/society/attempts-to-revive-education-sector-in-northern-syria.html |access-date=2024-12-02 |language=en-US}}</ref> In 2022, Ahmed al-Sharaa stated that 200,000 people in the SSG's territories had dropped out of school, warning this could lead to "illiteracy that leads to ignorance, which is a precursor to crime, unemployment, begging, and many other things."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Zelin |first=Aaron Y. |date=2023-01-06 |title=HTS's Self-Proclaimed Accomplishments in 2022 |url=https://syrianjihadism.com/2023/01/05/htss-self-proclaimed-accomplishments-in-2022/ |access-date=2024-12-02 |website=Syrian Jihadism |language=en}}</ref>

In September 2024, the [[White Helmets (Syria)|White Helmets]] reported that 170 schools had been bombed since 2019. In October 2023, the [[United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs|United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA)]] estimated that at least 1 million of the 2.2 million school-aged children in the region were not in education. In 2024, OCHA stated that a decline in international funding had deprived 700 schools of resources, affecting 110,000 students and 6,500 teachers.<ref name=":12" />
