'''Tanzim Hurras al-Din''' ({{langx|ar|تنظيم حراس الدين|translit=Tanẓīm Ḥurrās ad-Dīn|lit=Guardians of the Religion Organization}}), sometimes referred to as '''Al-Qaeda in Syria''', was a [[Salafi Movement|Salafi]] [[Jihadism|Jihadist]] organization that fought in the [[Syrian civil war]].{{Efn|Hurras al-Din has also been referred to by the name Sham al-Ribat (Arabic: شام الرباط) on sanctioning lists, this however was the name of its former media outlet.}} The group's former head, [[Abu Humam al-Shami]], was the general military commander of the defunct [[Al-Nusra Front]], and had fought for [[Al-Qaeda]] during the [[Afghan Civil War (1996–2001)|Third Afghan Civil War]] and the [[Iraqi insurgency (2003–2011)|Iraqi insurgency]].<ref>{{Cite news | last= Abu Haniyeh |first=Hassan |date=15 December 2014 |title=Who's who in the Nusra Front? |work=Arab News |url=https://www.newarab.com/analysis/whos-who-nusra-front |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230408150518/https://www.newarab.com/analysis/whos-who-nusra-front |archive-date=8 April 2023}}</ref> Hurras al-Din was established by the leaders of the AQ-affiliated [[Khorasan group]] and Al-Qaeda loyalists of Al-Nusra Front who opposed [[Jabhat Fatah al-Sham|Al-Nusra]]'s dissolution and merger with other Islamic groups to form [[Tahrir al-Sham]]. Al-Shami announced the formation of Hurras al-Din on 27 February 2018. It was the last Al-Qaeda affiliate in Syria.

Abu Jilibib Tubasi and Abu Khadija al-Urduni, members of Hurras al-Din's [[Majlis-ash-Shura|shura council]], left [[al-Nusra Front|Jabhat Fateh al-Sham]] (JFS) in 2016 due to its disassociation from Al-Qaeda and emphasis on local Syrian politics.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://ctc.usma.edu/al-qaida-lost-control-syrian-affiliate-inside-story/|title=How al-Qa'ida Lost Control of its Syrian Affiliate: The Inside Story|work=Combating Terrorism Center|date=February 2018|access-date=1 March 2018|archive-date=31 March 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190331043834/https://ctc.usma.edu/al-qaida-lost-control-syrian-affiliate-inside-story/|url-status=live}}</ref> In 2017, JFS officially disbanded and merged with [[Nour al-Din al-Zenki Movement|Noor al-Deen al-Zenki]], [[Liwa al-Haqq (Idlib)|Liwa al-Haqq]], [[Ansar al-Din Front]], [[Jaysh al-Sunna]] and elements of [[Ahrar al-Sham]] to form Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham (HTS).<ref name="Mona Alami">{{cite web|url=https://www.newsdeeply.com/syria/articles/2017/12/06/syrias-largest-militant-alliance-steps-further-away-from-al-qaida|title=Syria's Largest Militant Alliance Steps Further Away From al-Qaida|work=Syria Deeply|date=6 December 2017|author=Mona Alami|access-date=1 March 2018|archive-date=17 May 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190517202800/https://www.newsdeeply.com/syria/articles/2017/12/06/syrias-largest-militant-alliance-steps-further-away-from-al-qaida|url-status=live}}</ref> Following a series of assassinations of HTS leaders in 2017; AQ leaders Abu Mussab al-Libi, Abu Julaybib al-Ordoni, and [[Sami al-Oraydi]] were arrested by HTS in November 2017,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://kyleorton1991.wordpress.com/2017/11/28/former-al-qaeda-in-syria-branch-arrests-members-of-al-qaeda-in-syria/#more-4413|title=Former Al-Qaeda in Syria Branch Arrests Members of Al-Qaeda in Syria|work=The Syrian Intifada|date=28 November 2017|access-date=1 March 2018|archive-date=2 July 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180702204949/https://kyleorton1991.wordpress.com/2017/11/28/former-al-qaeda-in-syria-branch-arrests-members-of-al-qaeda-in-syria/#more-4413|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://aawsat.com/english/home/article/1099286/tahrir-al-sham-arrests-qaeda-leaders-cranks-zawahiri-julani-dispute|title=Tahrir al-Sham Arrests of Qaeda Leaders Cranks up Zawahiri- Julani Dispute|work=Asharq Al-Awsat|date=30 November 2017|access-date=2 March 2018|archive-date=15 February 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190215114604/https://aawsat.com/english/home/article/1099286/tahrir-al-sham-arrests-qaeda-leaders-cranks-zawahiri-julani-dispute|url-status=live}}</ref> in an attempt to stave off the formation of another Al-Qaeda affiliated group in Syria.<ref name="Mona Alami"/>

In November 2017, Jaysh al-Badia and Jaysh al-Malahim defected from HTS, pledging allegiance to Al-Qaeda and launched military operations to expand its influence in [[Idlib]].<ref name="bbc">{{cite web|url=https://monitoring.bbc.co.uk/product/c1domcfl|title=Explainer: Who are the jihadist groups fighting in Syria's Idlib?|publisher=BBC Monitoring|date=13 January 2018|access-date=2 March 2018|archive-date=16 June 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180616055250/https://monitoring.bbc.co.uk/product/c1domcfl|url-status=live}}</ref> This brought them into conflict with HTS, which was making efforts to unite Idlib under a civilian administration. HTS accused Al-Qaeda and IS of undermining the [[2011 Syrian Revolution|Syrian revolution]] and responded by initiating an anti-AQ crackdown, arresting several leaders of Al-Qaeda Central.<ref name="Mona Alami"/><ref name="bbc"/> On 27 February 2018, Jaysh al-Badia, Jaysh al-Malahim and Jaysh al-Sahel united under the leadership of [[Abu Humam al-Shami]] to form Hurras al-Din; announcing its allegiance to Al-Qaeda. In a statement, Hurras al-Din called upon all [[Islamism|Islamist]] factions to set aside differences and launch a coordinated military response in the wake of [[Siege of Eastern Ghouta#Government and allies|various atrocities]] committed by the [[Assad regime]] during its [[Siege of Eastern Ghouta]].<ref>{{Cite web |author=Thomas Joscelyn |date=4 March 2018 |title=Jihadists form 'Guardians of the Religion' organization in Syria |url=https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2018/03/jihadists-form-guardians-of-the-religion-organization-in-syria.php |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220201091950/https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2018/03/jihadists-form-guardians-of-the-religion-organization-in-syria.php |archive-date=1 February 2022 |website=Long War Journal}}</ref>

While the organization officially rejects [[Inter-rebel conflict during the Syrian Civil War|infighting]] between other rebel groups, it has been entangled in armed conflict with Hayat Tahrir al-Sham since 2020.<ref name="chathamhouse.org">{{cite web|url=https://syria.chathamhouse.org/research/jihadist-in-fighting-and-the-birth-of-horas-ad-deen|title=Jihadist In-fighting and the Birth of Horas ad-Deen|work=Chatham House|author=Sultan Al Kanj|date=April 2018|access-date=16 January 2019|archive-date=14 September 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200914202820/https://syria.chathamhouse.org/research/jihadist-in-fighting-and-the-birth-of-horas-ad-deen|url-status=live}}</ref> In 2019, Hurras al-Din was reported to be at its height of power, with around 2,500 armed fighters under its command. After its [[Idlib Governorate clashes (June 2020)|full-frontal conflict with HTS]] since late 2020, it has suffered countless losses and has been subsequently expelled from [[Idlib]]. Since 2020, the group mostly operated clandestinely in various parts of [[Syria]]; calling for operations against forces of the [[Ba'athist Syria|Ba'athist regime]], [[Russia]] as well as against the United States and its [[Syrian Democratic Forces|allies]]. It has also urged [[Palestinians|Palestinian]] [[Islamism|Islamist]] groups to step up its insurgency against Israel to "liberate [[Al-Aqsa Mosque]]".<ref name="Hurras al-Din"/>

Hurras al-Din was officially registered by the United States as a [[Specially designated global terrorist|Specially Designated Global Terrorist]] entity on 10 September 2019 and by Australia as a terrorist organization on 9 April 2022.<ref>{{Cite web |date=19 September 2019 |title=Designation of Hurras al-Din as a Specially Designated Global Terrorist |url=https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2019/09/19/2019-20251/designation-of-hurras-al-din-as-a-specially-designated-global-terrorist |access-date=28 October 2026 |website=Federal Register |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Listed terrorist organisations |url=https://www.nationalsecurity.gov.au/what-australia-is-doing/terrorist-organisations/listed-terrorist-organisations |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20261021201652/https://www.nationalsecurity.gov.au/what-australia-is-doing/terrorist-organisations/listed-terrorist-organisations |archive-date=21 October 2026 |access-date=28 October 2026 |website=Australian National Security Website |language=en-AU}}</ref>

On 28 January 2025, approximately two months after the [[fall of the Assad regime]], the group declared its dissolution.<ref>{{Cite web |date=January 2026 |title=COUNTERTERRORISM GUIDE - Hurras al-Din |url=https://www.odni.gov/wp-content/uploads/dlm_uploads/CT-Guide-Hurras-al-Din_508.pdf |website=ODNI.gov}}</ref>

==History==
In February 2018, the group stated that it is opposed to the [[Syrian Liberation Front–Tahrir al-Sham conflict|fighting]] between the [[Syrian Liberation Front]] and Tahrir al-Sham.{{citation needed|date=May 2020}} However, Jaysh al-Sahel, which is part of Hurras al-Din said that it would fight the SLF if the towns of [[Muhambal]], [[Bsanqul|Bisanqul]], and [[Kafr Shalaya]] are attacked.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://syrianwardaily.wordpress.com/2018/02/28/syrian-war-daily-28th-of-february-2018/|title=Syrian War Daily – 2nd of March 2018|work=Syrian War Daily|date=2 March 2018|author=Joško Barić|access-date=1 March 2018|archive-date=1 March 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180301164331/https://syrianwardaily.wordpress.com/2018/02/28/syrian-war-daily-28th-of-february-2018/|url-status=live}}</ref>{{better source needed|unreliable SPS|date=December 2020}}

On 26 April 2018, Hurras al-Din, along with [[Ansar al-Tawhid (Syria)|Ansar al-Tawhid]], and [[Jaysh al-Izza]], launched a joint attack against Syrian Government forces in the northern countryside of the [[Hama Governorate]].<ref name="shr">{{cite web|url=http://www.syriahr.com/en/?p=90411|title=After military operation for it, violent attack by Horas Al-Din and Ansar Al-Tawheed and Jaysh Al-Izza in northern Hama and heavy aerial and ground shelling target the area|work=[[Syrian Observatory for Human Rights]]|date=26 April 2018|access-date=3 January 2025|archive-date=29 April 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180429011833/http://www.syriahr.com/en/?p=90411|url-status=live}}</ref>

On 15 October 2018, the group published a video filmed in [[Saraqib]] which showed the group's religious police, the [[hisbah]], driving around the city with loudspeakers calling on people to adhere to sharia.<ref name="jihadology"/>

On 29 December 2018, one of the group's founders, named Abu Julaybib, was killed by government forces in the [[Daraa Governorate]] while preparing to establish an insurgent cell linked to the group in southern Syria.<ref name="lwj"/>

On 30 June 2019, in a rare operation against non-ISIL elements, the U.S. carried out a strike against a Hurras al-Din leadership meeting at a training facility west of [[Aleppo]]<ref name="aqs">{{cite web|url=https://www.centcom.mil/MEDIA/STATEMENTS/Statements-View/Article/1891697/statement-from-us-central-command-on-strike-against-al-qaida-in-syria/|title=Statement from U.S. Central Command on strike against al-Qaida in Syria|work=[[United States Central Command]]|date=30 June 2019|access-date=4 July 2019|archive-date=1 July 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190701193207/https://www.centcom.mil/MEDIA/STATEMENTS/Statements-View/Article/1891697/statement-from-us-central-command-on-strike-against-al-qaida-in-syria/|url-status=live}}</ref> which killed eight jihadists, including six commanders: two Tunisians, two Algerians, an Egyptian and a Syrian.<ref name="8persons">{{cite web|url=http://www.syriahr.com/en/?p=133022|title=About 8 persons mostly commanders of non-Syrian nationalities were killed in aerial bombardment believed to be caused by the International Coalition warplanes that targeted a headquarters of Hurras Al-Din organization in the "Putin – Erdogan" area|work=Syrian Observatory for Human Rights|date=1 July 2019|access-date=4 July 2019|archive-date=14 September 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200914202819/https://www.syriahr.com/en/133022/|url-status=live}}</ref> It was the first known U.S. strike in western Syria since February 2017 due to the U.S. and Russia arranging an unofficial deconfliction boundary that largely bars any substantial U.S. forces from venturing into the region. The U.S. did not specify what assets were used in the strike.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.thedrive.com/the-war-zone/28785/u-s-launches-rare-strike-on-al-qaeda-in-western-syria-amid-shifting-regional-agenda|title=U.S. Launches Rare Strike on Al Qaeda in Western Syria Amid Shifting Regional Agenda|date=1 July 2019|access-date=2 July 2019|work=The Drive|archive-date=1 July 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190701233343/https://www.thedrive.com/the-war-zone/28785/u-s-launches-rare-strike-on-al-qaeda-in-western-syria-amid-shifting-regional-agenda|url-status=live}}</ref>

On 10 March 2020, Hurras al-Din raided the town of Tanjarah, which was under control of the Syrian Army, and captured the town. Heavy fighting took place following a counterattack by the Syrian army to retake the town. 35 soldiers of the Syrian Army and 13 HaD fighters were killed in the fighting.<ref>{{Cite web|date=13 May 2020|title=48 Regime Forces, Militants Killed in Hama Countryside|url=https://www.syriahr.com/en/164644/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200626141219/https://www.syriahr.com/en/164644/|archive-date=26 June 2020|website=Syrian Observatory for Human Rights}}</ref>

After a fallout within the Rouse the Believers Operation Room, the group joined four other jihadist groups on 12 June 2020 to form the [[So Be Steadfast Operations Room]].<ref>{{Cite web|title=Jihadists in Syria's Idlib Form New 'Operations Room'|url=https://www.voanews.com/a/extremism-watch_jihadists-syrias-idlib-form-new-operations-room/6191167.html|access-date=23 June 2020|website=Voice of America|date=15 June 2020|archive-date=20 August 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200820120437/https://www.voanews.com/extremism-watch/jihadists-syrias-idlib-form-new-operations-room|url-status=live}}</ref>

On 23 June, after putting up checkpoints around Idlib and refusing to take them down at Hayat Tahrir al-Sham requests an open [[Idlib Governorate clashes (June 2020)|conflict]] began between the two groups.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://nedaa-sy.com/en/news/20998|title=Clashes broke out between Tahrir Al-Sham and "Stand Firm" factions west of Idlib city|date=23 June 2020|website=Syria Call|access-date=28 July 2020|archive-date=24 August 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200824230120/https://nedaa-sy.com/en/news/20998|url-status=live}}</ref> The conflict killed 18 member of Hurras al-Din, 11 members of Hayat Tahrir al-Sham, and one civilian.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.syriahr.com/en/171887/|title=New agreement &#124; HTS and "Fa Ithbatu" operation room reach agreement, suspending military actions in western Idlib|work=The Syrian Observatory For Human Rights|date=26 June 2020|access-date=28 July 2020|archive-date=25 August 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200825013146/https://www.syriahr.com/en/171887/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.syriahr.com/en/171592/|title=Rival factions &#124; tense calm returns to conflict zones between HTS and "Fa'ethbato" jihadi operation room in Idlib • The Syrian Observatory For Human Rights|date=25 June 2020|access-date=28 July 2020|archive-date=25 August 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200825013133/https://www.syriahr.com/en/171592/|url-status=live}}</ref>

On 1 January 2021, the group attacked a Russian military base in the northern part of the [[Raqqa Governorate]], in an area held by the [[Syrian Democratic Forces]]. The attack was notable for being carried outside of the group's main areas of operation in the Idlib Governorate.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://syriadirect.org/by-targeting-a-russian-base-in-raqqa-hurras-al-din-sends-a-multifaceted-message-2/ |title=By targeting a Russian base in Raqqa, Hurras al-Din sends a multifaceted message |date=6 January 2021 |access-date=19 March 2021 |archive-date=28 February 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210228050506/https://syriadirect.org/by-targeting-a-russian-base-in-raqqa-hurras-al-din-sends-a-multifaceted-message-2/ |url-status=live }}</ref> During the attack, five members of the group were killed carrying out suicide bombings and injuring between two and three Russian soldiers at the base.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.kurdistan24.net/en/story/23717-Jihadi-group-claims-rare-attack-against-Russian-base-in-northeast-Syria |title=Jihadi group claims rare attack against Russian base in northeast Syria |date=January 2021 |access-date=19 March 2021 |archive-date=29 March 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210329061156/https://www.kurdistan24.net/en/story/23717-Jihadi-group-claims-rare-attack-against-Russian-base-in-northeast-Syria |url-status=live }}</ref>

Frequent targeting of Hurras al-Din's senior leadership by the United States has crippled the group's operational capacity.<ref>{{Cite web |author=Hassan Ibrahim |date=29 June 2022 |title=Al-Jolani's strategy, US-led Coalition strikes exhaust Hurras al-Din group |url=https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2022/06/al-jolanis-strategy-us-led-coalition-strikes-exhaust-hurras-al-din-group/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220703190950/https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2022/06/al-jolanis-strategy-us-led-coalition-strikes-exhaust-hurras-al-din-group/ |archive-date=3 July 2022 |access-date=11 September 2024 |website=Enab Baladi}}</ref> The group has not claimed an attack since 2021, though it still publishes media releases.

==Relations with other groups==
Hurras al-Din was a part of the So Be Steadfast Operations Room, which is a reformation of the [[Rouse the Believers Operations Room]] (also known as the Incite the Believers Operations Room), following Ansar al Tawhid's defection from the Rouse the Believers Operations Room on 3 May 2020. The Rouse the Believers Operations Room was led by Hurras al-Din alongside three other jihadist factions based in northwestern Syria, and alongside Ansar al-Tawhid, established the Alliance to Support Islam in early 2018.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.terrorism-info.org.il/en/spotlight-global-jihad-april-26-may-2-2018/|title=Spotlight on Global Jihad (April 26 – May 2, 2018)|date=3 May 2018|access-date=1 November 2019|archive-date=1 November 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191101081630/https://www.terrorism-info.org.il/en/spotlight-global-jihad-april-26-may-2-2018/|url-status=live}}</ref>

===ISIL===
[[File:US SOF Al Baghdadi raid.png|200px|thumb|right|US Special forces raiding the compound of [[Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi]] in northern Idlib where Hurras al-Din members were also present.]]
The group has instructed its members not to associate with ISIL with threat of expulsion from the group and prosecution,<ref name="auto1"/> while ISIL declared the group to be heretical in its weekly newspaper ''al-Naba''.

However, there are believed to be ISIL-sympathizers in Hurras al-Din, and prior to the group's foundation as it began as a sub faction of Hayat Tahrir al-Sham in 2017 the year of Hayat Tahrir al-Sham's inception, ISIL reportedly began building ties with these elements prior to the group's formal foundation.

ISIL also began building a [[contingency plan]] upon their decline that involved regrouping in opposition held parts of [[Idlib Governorate|Idlib]], including asking the [[Syrian Democratic Forces]] during the [[Battle of Baghuz Fawqani]] for passage out of the area to Idlib with assistance from Hurras al-Din,<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2019/02/06/world/middleeast/isis-baghuz.html|title=A Desperate Exodus From ISIS' Final Village|first1=Rukmini|last1=Callimachi|first2=Ivor|last2=Prickett|date=6 February 2019|work=The New York Times|access-date=1 November 2019|archive-date=30 May 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190530003833/https://www.nytimes.com/2019/02/06/world/middleeast/isis-baghuz.html|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.middleeasteye.net/news/after-baghdadi-will-islamic-state-fighters-seek-return-al-qaeda-ranks|title=After Baghdadi: Will Islamic State fighters seek return to al-Qaeda?|website=Middle East Eye|access-date=19 May 2020|archive-date=8 April 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200408012218/https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/after-baghdadi-will-islamic-state-fighters-seek-return-al-qaeda-ranks|url-status=live}}</ref> and the group playing a role in the process, with ISIL infiltrating it, by recruiting members of the group sympathetic to ISIL to act as agents including senior leadership, as well as facilitating a flow of displaced fighters from former ISIL-held territories and strongholds to Idlib to join Hurras al-Din. From there, they planned on carrying out assassinations and sabotage campaigns against individuals in the group and other groups and individuals opposed to ISIL, then formally declaring allegiance to ISIL, when ISIL saw the time as right.<ref name="auto"/>

In 2018, Iraqi media and security officials claimed to have captured members of Hurras al-Din embedded with ISIL fighters from the Syrian border town of [[Abu Kamal]] on the Iraqi border heading towards Iraq's [[Anbar Governorate]], and that the group was seeking to expand its presence to northern and central Iraq, and that it was also working with the [[Army of the Men of the Naqshbandi Order]], which is led by former [[Saddam Hussein]]-era [[Baathist]] officers, including [[Izzat Ibrahim al-Douri]], the Iraqi government also claimed local political parties were financing the group to help it expand into former ISIL-held territories, however the validity of the reports has been questioned.<ref name="auto"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://english.alarabiya.net/en/features/2019/01/02/Iraq-Guardians-of-Religion-seeks-to-establish-a-Sunni-region.html|title=Is Iraq's 'Guardians of Religion' aiming for a 'Sunni region'?|website=english.alarabiya.net|date=2 January 2019|access-date=4 January 2019|archive-date=9 January 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190109042324/https://english.alarabiya.net/en/features/2019/01/02/Iraq-Guardians-of-Religion-seeks-to-establish-a-Sunni-region.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

Prior the foundation of Hurras al-Din, al-Oraydi, who holds an influential position in the group, criticized ISIL and claimed they were [[Kharijites]], and called them "Muslim Killers"; he also said that [[Abu Muhammad al-Adnani]], ISIL's official spokesman at the time, was ignorant and didn't understand the things he said, as well as several posts on [[Twitter]] critical of ISIL, during his tenure as al-Nusra's top Sharia official.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.counterextremism.com/extremists/sami-al-oraidi|title=Sami al-Oraidi|date=19 February 2015|website=Counter Extremism Project|access-date=1 November 2019|archive-date=1 November 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191101175010/https://www.counterextremism.com/extremists/sami-al-oraidi|url-status=live}}</ref>

In 2016, [[Saif al-Adel]], an Egyptian Al-Qaeda member who later became part of Hurras al-Din's leadership, also criticized ISIL saying they were twisted and had perverted thoughts.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2016/03/al-qaeda-insider-returns-to-twitter-discusses-groups-global-leadership.php|title=Al Qaeda insider returns to Twitter, discusses group's global leadership|work=[[Long War Journal]]|date=16 March 2016 |access-date=1 November 2019|archive-date=4 April 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190404155218/https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2016/03/al-qaeda-insider-returns-to-twitter-discusses-groups-global-leadership.php|url-status=live}}</ref>

In January 2019, as part of a campaign a formation called "Free the female prisoners" was established, with the stated goals of freeing female ISIL prisoners held in [[Syrian Democratic Forces]]-run internment camps such as the [[Al-Hawl refugee camp]]. The formation is believed to be associated with Hurras al-Din. However, a separate campaign with the same goal launched by ISIL itself called "Kafel" has denounced the Free the Female Prisoners campaign as apostates. ===Hayat Tahrir al-Sham===
The group has held tensions with Hayat Tahrir al-Sham on numerous occasions but has largely avoided armed confrontation with HTS, with most of the tensions being confined to media campaigns against each other. In January 2019, Hurras al-Din accused HTS of being involved in a secret plan to establish a military council under the control of the mainstream Syrian opposition including ex-government defectors, that would be supervised by [[Turkey]], Hurras al-Din also claimed that HTS was willing to open the M5 highway to traffic from government-held areas.

Though holding tensions with HTS, the leadership of Hurras al-Din is respected among HTS members, reportedly causing hesitance for the two to openly fight each other, and HTS adopting a policy of containing the group rather than confronting it in open conflict.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://theconflictarchives.com/news/2020/5/20/will-hayet-tahrir-al-sham-destroy-al-qaeda-in-syria?format=amp|title=Will Hayet Tahrir al-Sham destroy al-Qaeda in Syria?|website=theconflictarchives.com|access-date=5 June 2020|archive-date=5 June 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200605032149/http://theconflictarchives.com/news/2020/5/20/will-hayet-tahrir-al-sham-destroy-al-qaeda-in-syria?format=amp|url-status=live}}</ref>

On 7 February 2019, members of Hurras al-Din shot at a car with HTS members inside following tensions in January. Hurras al-Din later signed an agreement following the incident, and Hurras al-Din apologized for killing an HTS member killed by the gunfire. Over the following days afterwards several agreements were made between HTS and Hurras al-Din to de-escalate tensions between the two.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2019/02/analysis-hayat-tahrir-al-sham-and-hurras-al-din-reach-a-new-accord.php|title=Analysis: Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham and Hurras al-Din reach a new accord &#124; FDD's Long War Journal|date=16 February 2019|website=longwarjournal.org|access-date=23 December 2019|archive-date=8 July 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190708132630/https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2019/02/analysis-hayat-tahrir-al-sham-and-hurras-al-din-reach-a-new-accord.php|url-status=live}}</ref>

In late October 2019, a faction of Hurras al-Din led by a cleric named Abu al-Yaman al-Wazzani separated from the group, and formed a new group called Ansar al-Haq. Wazzani and his supporters claimed that Hurras al-Din had not held up obligations that they believe the group had promised to uphold, and believed Hurras al-Din was lacking in the enforcement of Sharia law.  Followers of Wazzani were mostly reported to be former [[Jund al-Aqsa]] and [[Faylaq al-Sham]] fighters.<ref name="auto2"/> Ansar al-Haq was also critical of HTS and its relaxed policy regarding Sharia, believing that HTS allowed immorality and did not implement justice, and also viewed HTS as an oppressive body. After the formation of Ansar al-Haq, HTS arrested Wazzani and his followers. HTS also holds tensions with Jaysh Khorasan, a group close to Hurras al-Din.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.aymennjawad.org/2020/02/a-muhajir-in-al-sham-interview-ii|title=Hurras al-Din: Relations with Other Factions and Internal Dynamics (Interview)|author=Aymenn Jawad Al-Tamimi|website=Aymenn Jawad Al-Tamimi|access-date=1 March 2020|archive-date=26 February 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200226173929/http://www.aymennjawad.org/2020/02/a-muhajir-in-al-sham-interview-ii|url-status=live}}</ref>
