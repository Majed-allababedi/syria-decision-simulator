'''Ahmed Hussein al-Sharaa'''{{efn|{{langx|ar|أحمَد حُسين الشرع|ʾAḥmad Ḥusayn ash-Sharaʿ}}, {{IPA|ar|ˈʔaħmad ħuˈsajn aʃˈʃaraʕ|pron}}. Also transliterated as '''Ahmad al-Sharaa''' and '''<nowiki>Ahmed al-Shara'</nowiki>'''.}} (born 29 October 1982), also known by his former ''[[nom de guerre]]''{{efn|name=nomdeguerre}} '''Abu Mohammad al-Julani''',{{efn|{{langx|ar|أبو محمد الجولاني|ʾAbū Muḥammad al-Jawlānī|links=no}}, {{IPA|ar|ˈʔabuː muˈħammad al.dʒawˈlaːniː|pron}}. Also transliterated as '''Jolani''', '''Joulani''', '''Jawlani''', and '''Golani'''.}} is a Syrian statesman and former rebel commander who has served as the [[president of Syria]] since 2025. As the [[emir]] of [[Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham]] (HTS) and the de facto leader of the [[Syrian Salvation Government]] (SSG) during the [[Syrian civil war]], he led the decisive [[2024 Syrian opposition offensives|Syrian opposition offensives]] that resulted in the [[fall of the Assad regime]] in 2024.

Born in [[Riyadh]], Saudi Arabia, to a Syrian [[Sunni Islam|Sunni Muslim]] family from the [[Golan Heights]], he grew up in Syria's capital, [[Damascus]]. Al-Sharaa joined [[al-Qaeda in Iraq]] shortly before the [[2003 invasion of Iraq|2003 U.S. invasion of Iraq]] and fought for two years in the [[Iraqi insurgency (2003–2011)|Iraqi insurgency]]. From 2005 to 2011, he was captured and imprisoned by American and Iraqi forces. His release coincided with the onset of the [[Syrian revolution]] against the [[Ba'athist Syria|Ba'athist regime]] of [[Bashar al-Assad]]. In 2012, with the support of [[al-Qaeda|al-Qaeda's]] central command, al-Sharaa founded [[Al-Nusra Front|Jabhat al-Nusra]], also known as al-Nusra Front, to depose the Assad regime.

As the emir of al-Nusra Front, al-Sharaa built a stronghold in [[Idlib Governorate]] during the Syrian civil war, although his forces committed a number of war crimes against civilians. Al-Sharaa resisted [[Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi]]'s attempts to merge al-Nusra Front with the [[Islamic State]], leading to total war between the two groups. In 2016, al-Sharaa cut Jabhat al-Nusra's ties with al-Qaeda, launched a crackdown on its loyalists, and rebranded his organisation as [[Al-Nusra Front#As Jabhat Fatah Al-Sham (July 2016 – January 2017)|Jabhat Fatah al-Sham]]. After breaking with al-Qaeda, he sought international legitimacy by presenting a more moderate view of himself, renouncing transnational [[jihadism]] and focusing on governance in Syria. 

In 2017, al-Sharaa merged Jabhat Fatah al-Sham with other rebel groups to create Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham, which he led until 2025. HTS established the Syrian Salvation Government, a [[Technocracy|technocratic]] civilian administration, in the territory it controlled in Idlib Governorate. The SSG collected taxes and provided public services, though it faced protests and criticism within Idlib for authoritarian tactics and suppressing dissent. In November 2024, al-Sharaa launched a major [[2024 Syrian opposition offensives|11-day offensive]] against the Assad regime that culminated in the [[Fall of Damascus (2024)|capture of Damascus]] by opposition forces, [[Fall of the Assad regime|toppling the Ba'athist regime]] as Bashar al-Assad fled to Russia.

Al-Sharaa became Syria's de facto [[List of heads of state of Syria|head of state]], leading the [[Syrian caretaker government]] from 8 December 2024 until 29 January 2025, when he was formally appointed to the Syrian presidency. As president of [[Syrian transitional government|Syria's transitional government]], he focused on consolidating power, rebuilding state institutions, unifying armed factions, and restoring [[Foreign relations of Syria|foreign relations]], including with the [[Syria–United States relations|United States]], [[Russia–Syria relations|Russia]], and other regional powers. He signed a [[Constitutional Declaration of the Syrian Arab Republic|constitutional declaration]] establishing a five-year transitional period that enshrined [[Fiqh|Islamic jurisprudence]] as the main source of legislation with plans to hold [[Next Syrian presidential election|elections]] afterwards. In October 2025, a [[2025 Syrian parliamentary election|parliamentary election]] was held under al-Sharaa's administration, the first since Assad's overthrow.<ref>{{cite news |title=Syria holds first elections since Assad, but not in all provinces|url=https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/czrpx1vvr45o|access-date=6 October 2025|publisher=BBC|date=5 October 2025}}</ref> 

Domestically, al-Sharaa has pursued economic recovery, security stabilisation, transitional justice, and the [[Return of refugees of the Syrian civil war|return of Syrian refugees]]. Extensive negotiations with the Kurdish-led [[Syrian Democratic Forces]] (SDF) to integrate their organisation into the state remained inconclusive throughout 2025. In January 2026, after [[Aleppo clashes (2025–2026)|clashes in Aleppo]] between the transitional government and SDF forces, he signed a decree stating that [[Kurds in Syria|Syrian Kurds]] are an essential part of Syria while recognising the [[Kurdish language]] and [[Newroz as celebrated by Kurds|Newroz]]. His government [[2026 northeastern Syria offensive|launched a successful offensive]] against the SDF and subsequently signed a peace and integration agreement, culminating in the full integration of its [[Democratic Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria|civil institutions]] and the dissolution of the SDF in August 2026.  

In the first year of [[Presidency of Ahmed al-Sharaa|al-Sharaa's presidency]], [[2025 massacres of Syrian Alawites|massacres]] targeting [[Alawites]] took place in coastal Syria, while [[Southern Syria clashes (July–September 2025)|clashes]] with the [[Druze]] occurred in southern Syria, both involving government-affiliated troops. Following the fall of the Ba'athist regime, [[Israel]] called the [[Agreement on Disengagement between Israel and Syria|1974 Agreement on Disengagement]] void, and initiated a [[Israeli invasion of Syria (2024–present)|limited invasion]] of southwestern Syria from the [[Israeli occupation of the Golan Heights|Israeli-occupied Golan Heights]]. Al-Sharaa has opposed renewed conflict with Israel, reaffirmed Syria's commitment to the 1974 deal, and condemned Iranian influence.

==Syrian civil war==
=== Syrian uprising and foundation of al-Nusra Front ===
During a routine detention review, Iraqi authorities, finding no charges against al-Sharaa's pseudonym, Amjad Mudhafar, released him on 13 March 2011, days before the beginning of the [[Syrian revolution]].<ref name="nyt">{{Cite news |last=Hubbard |first=Ben |date=23 September 2025 |title=The Many Faces of Syria's President, Ahmed al-Shara |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/23/world/middleeast/syria-president-ahmed-al-shara.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250923123906/https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/23/world/middleeast/syria-president-ahmed-al-shara.html |archive-date=23 September 2025 |access-date=23 September 2025 |work=[[The New York Times]] |pages=0362–4331 |language=en}}</ref> In August 2011, al-Sharaa was tasked by al-Qaeda emir [[Ayman al-Zawahiri]] to establish al-Qaeda's mission in Syria.<ref name="aljazeera20241204" /><ref>{{Cite book |last=Klausen |first=Jytte |title=Western Jihadism: A Thirty-Year History |publisher=Oxford University Press |year=2021 |isbn=978-0-19-887079-1 |location=Great Clarendon Street, Oxford, OX2 6DP, United Kingdom |pages=399 |chapter=11: The ISIS Effect |quote=In August 2011, the new emir of Al Qaeda, Bin Laden's old second-in-command, Ayman al-Zawahiri and Al Qaeda's central command sent Abu Mohammad al-Julani to set up a mission in Syria. |access-date= |chapter-url= }}</ref> Alongside senior al-Qaeda operatives, he formed ''[[Al-Nusra Front|Jabhat an-Nuṣrah li-Ahl ash-Shām]]'' (<small>[[Literal translation|lit.]] </small>'Front of the Supporters of the People of the Levant') which was envisioned as a broad coalition of Islamist militant groups in Syria, led by al-Sharaa with direct allegiance to al-Qaeda's central command.<ref name="frontline">{{Cite web |title=The Jihadist |url=https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/frontline/documentary/the-jihadist/? |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210607035634/https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/frontline/film/the-jihadist/ |archive-date=7 June 2021 |access-date=7 June 2021 |website=[[PBS]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |last=Klausen |first=Jytte |title=Western Jihadism: A Thirty-Year History |publisher=Oxford University Press |year=2021 |isbn=978-0-19-887079-1 |location=Great Clarendon Street, Oxford, OX2 6DP, United Kingdom |pages=399 |chapter=11: The ISIS Effect |quote=Al-Julani and a cadre of experienced operatives from the Al Qaeda high command set up ''Jabhat al-Nusra'', also known in English as the Nusra Front, which was supposed to function as a united front organization for the various jihadist fighter groups. ... Despite later tensions with the leadership of the [[Islamic State of Iraq]] (ISI), al-Sharaa proceeded to orchestrate an agreement with its emir [[Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi]], who gave him [[United States dollar|$]]50,000 (approximately 60 million [[Iraqi dinar|dinar]]) to expand Jabhat al-Nusra as [[al-Qaeda]]'s Syrian branch.<ref name="nyt" /> ISI provided al-Sharaa with fighters, weapons and funding, and maintained an alliance with Jabhat al-Nusra until 2013, with disputes referred to mediation by al-Qaeda emir Ayman al-Zawahiri.<ref>{{cite web |last=Hassan |first=Hassan |title=Two Houses Divided: How Conflict in Syria Shaped the Future of Jihadism |url=https://ctc.westpoint.edu/two-houses-divided-conflict-syria-shaped-future-jihadism/ |publisher=Combating Terrorism Center at West Point |work=CTC Sentinel |date=October 2018}}</ref> Sources differ on whether al-Sharaa was the one who came up with the idea of forming Jabhat al-Nusra or another leader in ISI; however, what is certain is that al-Sharaa became the "general emir" of Jabhat al-Nusra when it was officially announced in January 2012.<ref>{{cite web |last=Lister |first=Charles |title=Profiling the Islamic State |url=https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/brookings-analysis-paper_charles-lister_web.pdf |publisher=Brookings Institution |date=2016}}</ref>

=== Split from al-Qaeda ===
The Russian entrance into the war led to military setbacks for the Syrian opposition, causing their foreign backers to exert a greater level of influence over them. In January 2016, al-Nusra Front held unity negotiations with other rebel groups in a bid to pre-empt any foreign attempt to co-opt these groups against it. When the talks collapsed due to concerns over Jabhat al-Nusra's affiliation with al-Qaeda, the reformist wing of al-Nusra Front, which may have comprised a third of its overall membership, presented al-Sharaa with an ultimatum: to sever ties with al-Qaeda and merge with other rebel groups, or face a mass defection.<ref name="westpoint20180215">{{Cite web |last=Lister |first=Charles |date=15 February 2018 |title=How al-Qa'ida Lost Control of its Syrian Affiliate: The Inside Story |url=https://ctc.westpoint.edu/al-qaida-lost-control-syrian-affiliate-inside-story/ |access-date=19 December 2024 |website=[[Combating Terrorism Center|Combating Terrorism Center at West Point]] |language=en-US |archive-date=4 May 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220504095122/https://ctc.westpoint.edu/al-qaida-lost-control-syrian-affiliate-inside-story/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

In July 2016, al-Sharaa convened Jabhat al-Nusra's [[Majlis al-Shura|Shura Council]] twice to discuss the matter. The first council was inconclusive, while the second council settled on a "middle way" after several meetings: to break ties with al-Qaeda outside of Syria while retaining them inside Syria.<ref name="westpoint20180215" /> As al-Zawahiri could not be contacted, several senior al-Qaeda leaders, including al-Zawahiri's deputy [[Abu Khayr al-Masri]], approved the split contingent on al-Zawahiri later approving it himself.<ref name="Zelin-2022-17-18">Zelin 2022, pp. 17–18</ref><ref name="westpoint20180215" /> If he did not, the split would have to be reversed. Al-Sharaa agreed to these terms, which Jabhat al-Nusra's Shura Council narrowly approved.<ref name="westpoint20180215" />

On 28 July 2016, al-Sharaa announced that al-Nusra Front had severed ties with al-Qaeda and rebranded as [[Al-Nusra Front#As Jabhat Fatah Al-Sham (July 2016 – January 2017)|Jabhat Fatah al-Sham]] (JFS).<ref name="Rebrand">{{Cite news |date=28 July 2016 |title=Syrian Nusra Front announces split from al-Qaeda |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-middle-east-36916606 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160730021539/https://www.bbc.com/news/world-middle-east-36916606 |archive-date=30 July 2016 |access-date=28 July 2016 |agency=[[BBC News]]}}</ref><ref name="split">{{Cite news |date=29 July 2016 |title=Al-Nusra leader Jolani announces split from al-Qaeda |url=https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2016/7/29/al-nusra-leader-jolani-announces-split-from-al-qaeda |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221203103759/https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2016/7/29/al-nusra-leader-jolani-announces-split-from-al-qaeda |archive-date=3 December 2022 |work=Al Jazeera}}</ref> He added that the new organisation would have "no affiliation to any external entity".<ref name="LWJ1">{{cite journal |date=28 July 2016 |title=Analysis: Al Nusrah Front rebrands itself as Jabhat Fath Al Sham |url=http://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2016/07/analysis-al-nusrah-front-rebrands-itself-as-jabhat-fath-al-sham.php |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190324200647/https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2016/07/analysis-al-nusrah-front-rebrands-itself-as-jabhat-fath-al-sham.php |archive-date=24 March 2019 |access-date=17 January 2017 |website=FDD's Long War Journal}}</ref> Ayman al-Zawahiri was publicly supportive of the split,<ref name="Rebrand" /> even though he had rejected the plan when it was presented to him.<ref name="Zelin-2022-17-18" /> In protest, several leading al-Qaeda loyalists in al-Nusra Front left JFS.<ref name="westpoint20180215" />

=== Formation of Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham (HTS) ===
After forming JFS, al-Sharaa attempted to arrange a merger with [[Ahrar al-Sham]].<ref name="Zelin-2022-18-19">Zelin 2022, pp. 18–19</ref> However, negotiations collapsed due to al-Sharaa supporting [[Jund al-Aqsa]], a group that he had secretly established to discourage Jabhat al-Nusra's foreign fighters from defecting to ISIS, in their [[October 2016 Idlib Governorate clashes|conflict]] with Ahrar al-Sham. Ahrar al-Sham's leadership was also concerned that JFS continued to maintain ties with al-Qaeda.<ref name="westpoint20180215" /> Meanwhile, al-Sharaa came under attack from al-Qaeda. [[Abu Muhammad al-Maqdisi]] wrote a critique of JFS, and rumours circulated that al-Qaeda was preparing to launch a new affiliate in Syria.<ref name="westpoint20180215" />

In a last-ditch attempt to secure a merger, al-Sharaa led efforts to undermine Ahrar al-Sham's nationalist and anti-merger wing.<ref name="westpoint20180215" /> When Ahrar al-Sham's leadership again refused to merge in December 2017, the Islamist and pro-merger wing formed a breakaway "sub-faction" named [[Jaysh al-Ahrar]].<ref name="Zelin-2022-18-19" /><ref name="westpoint20180215" /> Shortly afterwards, JFS attacked [[Free Syrian Army]] (FSA) positions across Idlib and Aleppo, precipitating a [[Idlib Governorate clashes (January–March 2017)|conflict]] with Ahrar al-Sham. The conflict allowed JFS to defeat CIA-backed FSA groups, which it viewed as a "foreign conspiracy".<ref name="westpoint20180215" />

On 28 January 2017, al-Sharaa announced that JFS would dissolve and merge with [[Liwa al-Haqq (Idlib)|Liwa al-Haqq]], [[Jaysh al-Sunna]], [[Ansar al-Din Front]] and the [[Nour al-Din al-Zenki Movement]] to form [[Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham]] (HTS),<ref name="new">{{cite web |last=Joscelyn |first=Thomas |date=28 January 2017 |title=Al Qaeda and allies announce 'new entity' in Syria |url=http://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2017/01/al-qaeda-and-allies-announce-new-entity-in-syria.php |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170529023948/http://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2017/01/al-qaeda-and-allies-announce-new-entity-in-syria.php |archive-date=29 May 2017 |access-date= |website=[[FDD's Long War Journal]] |publisher=Foundation for Defense of Democracies}}</ref><ref name="westpoint20180215" /> gaining approximately 3,000 to 5,000 more fighters.<ref name="westpoint20180215" /> In an effort to demonstrate the maturity of the new group, al-Sharaa arranged for Jaysh al-Ahrar's leader [[Abu Jaber Sheikh]] to serve as the nominal emir of HTS, although authority was formally transferred back to al-Sharaa by December 2017.<ref name="Zelin-2022-18-19" /> The remaining al-Qaeda loyalists in JFS viewed the formation of HTS as a definitive break from the organisation and refused to join. A number of al-Qaeda veterans publicly criticised al-Sharaa for his moves. As a result, several were arrested by HTS towards the end of 2017. After their release, some were involved in forming [[Hurras al-Din]], the new Syrian affiliate of al-Qaeda.<ref name="Zelin-2022-18-19" />

The U.S. government quickly rejected this rebranding, with the U.S. Embassy in Syria stating that "The core of HTS is Nusra, a designated terrorist organisation. This designation applies regardless of what name it uses or what groups merge into it." The Embassy characterised HTS's formation as an attempt to "hijack the Syrian revolution" rather than a move toward moderation.<ref name="ToI24">{{Cite web |title=US Stands Firm: HTS and Jolani Still Part of al-Qaeda Network |url=http://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/articleshow/116111130.cms |website=Times of India |access-date=7 December 2024 |archive-date=8 December 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241208163935/https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/articleshow/116111130.cms |url-status=live }}</ref> On 10 May 2017, the [[Rewards for Justice Program|Rewards for Justice program]] announced a $10 million reward for information leading to the identification or location of al-Sharaa, who was identified by the program as the leader of [[al-Nusra Front|Jabhat al-Nusra]].<ref>{{Cite web|title=Rewards for Justice - Reward Offer for Information on al-Nusrah Front Leader Muhammad al-Jawlani|url=https://2017-2021.state.gov/rewards-for-justice-reward-offer-for-information-on-al-nusrah-front-leader-muhammad-al-jawlani/|access-date=2026-05-29|website=United States Department of State|language=en-US|archive-date=10 May 2026|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260510233542/https://2017-2021.state.gov/rewards-for-justice-reward-offer-for-information-on-al-nusrah-front-leader-muhammad-al-jawlani/|url-status=live}}</ref> 
[[File:Mohammed al-Julani Reward poster.jpg|thumb|Redacted [[Rewards for Justice]] poster for al-Sharaa, with the photographic portrait removed due to copyright concerns.]]

Under al-Sharaa's leadership, [[Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham]] prioritised combating al-Qaeda and ISIS in an effort to improve its standing with Western nations. HTS successfully defeated ISIS, al-Qaeda, and most opposing forces in its territory, establishing control over most of [[Idlib Governorate]], which it governed through its civilian administration, the [[Syrian Salvation Government]] (SSG).<ref name="frontline" /><ref>{{cite web |date=2 October 2017 |title=Julani is a temporary leader of the "Liberation of the Sham" .. === Idlib governance ===
{{Main|Syrian Salvation Government}}

[[File:Syrian Civil War map (November 24, 2023).svg|thumb|Military situation before the opposition offensives in late 2024.<br />Territories held by [[Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham]] (white) and the Syrian government (red).]]
Under al-Sharaa's technocratic administration, Idlib experienced significant economic development, becoming Syria's fastest-growing region despite being historically its poorest province. The area featured new luxury shopping malls, housing estates, and a round-the-clock electricity supply surpassing that of the capital, Damascus. Educational facilities included a university with 18,000 segregated students. However, his government faced criticism for its taxation policies, including customs taxes on goods from Turkey and checkpoint fees on smuggled goods, as well as the economic impact of the Turkish lira's depreciation, which was the main currency in the region.<ref name= Econ24>{{cite news|title=Protests have erupted against another Syrian dictator|url=https://www.economist.com/middle-east-and-africa/2024/04/04/protests-have-erupted-against-another-syrian-dictator|newspaper=The Economist|date=4 April 2024|access-date=7 December 2024|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20240417004904/https://www.economist.com/middle-east-and-africa/2024/04/04/protests-have-erupted-against-another-syrian-dictator |archive-date=17 April 2024}}</ref><ref name= NYT24>{{cite news |last1=Rasgon|first1=Adam|last2=Abdulrahim|first2=Raja |date=8 December 2024 |title=Who Is the Leader of Syria's Rebel Offensive? |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/12/08/world/middleeast/syria-hts-jolani.html |archive-url=https://archive.today/20241208163410/https://www.nytimes.com/2024/12/08/world/middleeast/syria-hts-jolani.html |archive-date=8 December 2024 |access-date=18 December 2024 |work=[[The New York Times]]}}</ref>

In August 2018, al-Sharaa said that both HTS's allies and opponents viewed the group as "the greatest defender of [[Sunni Islam|Sunnis]] in Syria." He cautioned that Turkish positions were uncertain, as political stances could change anytime. He also defended the [[Siege of al-Fu'ah and Kafriya|evacuation of the Shia-majority villages of Foua and Kafraya]], saying it removed the threat of "sectarian militias" and prevented Iran from having a pretext to attack.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Syrian militant leader slams Turkey and defends evacuations in new video |url=https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/syrian-militant-leader-slams-turkey-and-defends-evacuations-new-video |access-date=21 February 2026 |website=Middle East Eye |language=en |archive-date=16 March 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260316022824/https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/syrian-militant-leader-slams-turkey-and-defends-evacuations-new-video |url-status=live }}</ref>

In February 2023, Idlib Governorate, which was under the control of the [[Syrian Salvation Government]], was one of the territories hardest hit by the [[2023 Turkey–Syria earthquakes|Turkey–Syria earthquakes]].<ref>{{Cite news |author1=Michaelson, Tondo |author2=Ruth, Lorenzo |date=13 February 2023 |title=Grief and desperation in Idlib as earthquake compounds crises |url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/2023/feb/13/grief-and-desperation-in-idlib-as-earthquake-compounds-crises |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230213190605/https://www.theguardian.com/world/2023/feb/13/grief-and-desperation-in-idlib-as-earthquake-compounds-crises |archive-date=13 February 2023 |work=The Guardian}}</ref> The Assad regime's policy of besieging northwestern Syria and blockading the supply of food, medicines and other humanitarian supplies further worsened the crisis in Idlib.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Ioanes |first=Ellen |date=12 February 2023 |title=Why the earthquake caused a 'perfect storm' of a crisis in Syria |url=https://www.vox.com/world/2023/2/12/23594720/earthquake-aid-crisis-syria-united-nations |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230214211353/https://www.vox.com/world/2023/2/12/23594720/earthquake-aid-crisis-syria-united-nations |archive-date=14 February 2023 |work=Vox}}</ref> Al-Sharaa criticised aid agencies for neglecting Idlib and urged the international community to step up reconstruction and relief efforts, saying, "The United Nations needs to understand that it is required to help in a crisis." When asked about efforts to rebrand HTS, al-Sharaa said, "We have built a government that meets our people's needs, but much more is still required."<ref name="SR">{{Cite news |author1=Michaelson, Tondo |author2=Ruth, Lorenzo |date=13 February 2023 |title=Syrian rebel leader pleads for outside help a week on from earthquakes |url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/2023/feb/13/syria-earthquake-rebel-leader-pleas-for-outside-help |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230214012142/https://www.theguardian.com/world/2023/feb/13/syria-earthquake-rebel-leader-pleas-for-outside-help |archive-date=14 February 2023 |work=The Guardian}}</ref>

In March 2024, widespread protests erupted in [[Idlib Governorate]] against al-Sharaa's rule, with demonstrators adopting the slogan "''Isqat al-Julani''" ("Down with Julani"), reminiscent of earlier protests against the Assad regime. Hundreds of protesters marched through Idlib's cities and towns for over a month. The protests were triggered by multiple factors, including allegations of brutality by HTS, with reports of thousands of critics held in prisons, and economic grievances related to high taxes.<ref name= Econ24/> In response, al-Sharaa made several concessions. He released hundreds of detainees from a previous summer's security operation, including his former deputy [[Abu Maria al-Qahtani]], who had been arrested along with 300 others in a [[purge]]. He also promised local elections and increased employment opportunities for displaced persons, while warning protesters against what he termed as treachery.<ref name= Econ24/>

Turkey, which had previously helped stabilize the province by connecting it to its electricity grid and allowing building materials to enter freely, had grown concerned about al-Sharaa's expanding influence. In response, it reduced trade through its border crossings with Idlib, affecting HTS's revenue. Reports indicated that al-Sharaa had twice attempted to take over territory from the [[Turkish occupation of northern Syria|Turkish-administered regions in northern Syria]].<ref name= Econ24/>

=== Easing sanctions on Syria ===
{{See also|International sanctions against Syria}}
Since 2011, several countries and international bodies have imposed sanctions on Syria under Bashar al-Assad's rule, mainly due to the Ba'athist regime's violent crackdown on civilians during the [[Syrian civil war]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Seidensticker |first=Hannah |date=12 February 2025 |title=Sanctions against Syria – |url=https://www.wiacon.de/en/sanctions-against-syria/ |access-date=5 July 2025 |website=WIACON |language=en-US |archive-date=10 July 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250710175155/https://www.wiacon.de/en/sanctions-against-syria/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Ousted Syrian regime's 'war criminals' faced global sanctions over years of civil war |url=https://www.aa.com.tr/en/americas/ousted-syrian-regimes-war-criminals-faced-global-sanctions-over-years-of-civil-war/3438965 |access-date=5 July 2025 |website=Anadolu Ajansi |archive-date=10 September 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250910114231/https://www.aa.com.tr/en/americas/ousted-syrian-regimes-war-criminals-faced-global-sanctions-over-years-of-civil-war/3438965 |url-status=live }}</ref> In an interview with ''The New York Times'' in April 2025, al-Sharaa stated that the sanctions should be permanently lifted, as they were imposed on the [[Ba'athist Syria|Assad regime]]. He noted that the sanctions were damaging his government and slowing economic recovery.<ref name="tnyt" />

In November 2025, with the support of the United States and other countries, the UN Security Council [[United Nations Security Council Resolution 2799|removed]] al-Sharaa and his Interior Minister [[Anas Khattab]] from the sanctions list of the [[ISIL (Da'esh) and Al-Qaida Sanctions Committee|ISIL and Al-Qaida Sanctions Committee]]. On 7 November, the United States also delisted him from its [[Specially Designated Global Terrorist]] (SDGT) sanctions list, while the United Kingdom announced the removal of both al-Sharaa and Anas Khattab from its consolidated sanctions list, stating that he was no longer subject to asset freezes.<ref>{{cite news |author1=Conor Lennon|date=6 November 2025|title=Security Council lifts terror-related sanctions on Syrian President|url=https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166303|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251107041124/https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166303|archive-date=7 November 2025|access-date=7 November 2025|publisher=UN News}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=US removes Syrian president from specifically designated global terrorist sanction list|url=https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/us-removes-syrian-president-from-specifically-designated-global-terrorist-sanction-list-/3738053|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251205220347/https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/us-removes-syrian-president-from-specifically-designated-global-terrorist-sanction-list-/3738053|archive-date=5 December 2025|access-date=5 December 2025|website=Anadolu Agency}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=7 November 2025|title=U.K. removes President al-Sharaa from sanctions list|url=https://sana.sy/en/syria/2276410/|access-date=20 February 2026|language=en-US}}</ref>

== Political positions ==
=== Views on Bashar al-Assad's handover to Syria===
{{See also|Proposed handover of Bashar al-Assad to Syria}}
In an interview with ''[[The New York Times]]'' in April 2025, al-Sharaa said that Syrian officials requested Russia to extradite Assad as a condition for allowing their military presence in Syria, but Russia refused.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Goldbaum |first=Christina |title=Syria's Jihadist-Turned-President Seeks New Allies |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2025/04/23/world/middleeast/syria-president-al-shara-interview.html |website=The New York Times |date=23 April 2025 |access-date=27 April 2025 |issn=0362-4331 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250423215541/https://www.nytimes.com/2025/04/23/world/middleeast/syria-president-al-shara-interview.html |archive-date=23 April 2025 |url-status=live}}</ref> In a September 2025 interview with ''[[60 Minutes]]'', al-Sharaa said he still intended to pursue justice against [[Bashar al-Assad]] through legal means.<ref>{{Cite web |date=12 October 2025 |title=Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa's journey from al Qaeda to rebuilding his country after rebellion - CBS News |url=https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ahmed-al-sharaa-syria-rebuilding-60-minutes/ |access-date=15 October 2025 |website=CBS News |archive-url=https://archive.today/20251015000842/https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ahmed-al-sharaa-syria-rebuilding-60-minutes/ |archive-date=15 October 2025 |url-status=live}}</ref>

In a ''[[Fox News]]'' interview on 10 November 2025, al-Sharaa said that Russia had a different view on sending wanted individuals back to Syria, but he stressed that justice must be carried out through a [[National Commission for Transitional Justice (Syria)|transitional justice committee]] to ensure accountability for all perpetrators, including Assad.<ref name="sana.sy">{{Cite web |date=11 November 2025 |title=President al-Sharaa to Fox News: Meeting Trump marks start of Syrian and U.S.relations - Syrian Arab News Agency (SANA) |url=https://sana.sy/en/politics/2277158/ |access-date=11 November 2025 |language=en-US |archive-date=12 November 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251112065230/https://sana.sy/en/politics/2277158/ |url-status=live }}</ref> In a November 2025 interview with ''[[The Washington Post]]'', al-Sharaa said that the issue of Bashar al-Assad remained sensitive for Russia, with Syria's relationship with Moscow still in its early stages and emphasised that "Syria will preserve its right to demand Assad's trial."<ref name="SANA12" />

In an interview with the ''[[BBC]]'', al-Sharaa discussed his plans and said that Syria is not like the [[Taliban]]. In his first interview as president on 9 February 2025, al-Sharaa told ''[[The Economist]]'' that he opposed the [[federalization of Syria|federalisation of Syria]]. He also mentioned that Turkey was planning a full-scale operation in the north against Kurdish forces but had asked them to wait to allow for [[negotiation]]s. In response to a question about whether [[Sharia]] could be implemented, he stated, "That decision rests with the experts. If they approve it, my duty is to enforce it; if they reject it, my duty is to uphold their decision as well." When asked if Syria would become a [[democracy]], he responded, "In our region, there are various definitions of democracy. If democracy means that the people decide who will rule them and who represents them in parliament, then yes, Syria is moving in that direction."<ref>{{Cite news |date=3 February 2025 |title=An interview with Ahmed al-Sharaa, Syria's president |url=https://www.economist.com/middle-east-and-africa/2025/02/03/an-interview-with-ahmed-al-sharaa-syrias-president |url-access=subscription |url-status=live |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250223041812/https://www.economist.com/middle-east-and-africa/2025/02/03/an-interview-with-ahmed-al-sharaa-syrias-president |archive-date=23 February 2025 |access-date=9 February 2025 |newspaper=The Economist |language=en-US}}</ref>

In an interview with ''The New York Times'' in April 2025, al-Sharaa stated that his government was considering granting citizenship to foreign fighters who had lived in Syria for many years and supported the revolution. Furthermore, he said that his government was negotiating with Turkey and Russia regarding their military presence in Syria and suggested that both countries might eventually provide military support to the new Syrian government.<ref name="tnyt">{{Cite news |last=Goldbaum |first=Christina |date=23 April 2025 |title=Syria's New President Talked to The New York Times. On 27 May 2025, during the "Aleppo, the Key to Victory" event celebrating the [[Syrian revolution]] and the [[Battle of Aleppo (2024)|liberation of Aleppo]], al-Sharaa exhorted the Syrian people to help reconstruct the country: "Our war against tyrants has ended, and our battle against poverty has begun."<ref>{{Cite news|title=Syria's Sharaa launches 'battle for reconstruction' in Aleppo |url=https://www.newarab.com/news/syrias-sharaa-launches-battle-reconstruction-aleppo |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250529031259/https://www.newarab.com/news/syrias-sharaa-launches-battle-reconstruction-aleppo |archive-date=29 May 2025 |access-date=2 June 2025 |work=The New Arab |language=en-EN |url-status=live }}</ref>

In a September 2025 interview with ''[[60 Minutes]]'', al-Sharaa said elections would be held once Syria's infrastructure and citizens' records are restored. He stated that he wants Syria to be a place [[Universal suffrage|where every person can vote]].<ref name="tnpp">{{Cite web |date=12 October 2025 |title=Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa's journey from al Qaeda to rebuilding his country after rebellion - CBS News |url=https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ahmed-al-sharaa-syria-rebuilding-60-minutes/ |access-date=13 October 2025 |website=www.cbsnews.com |language=en-US}}</ref> He added that rebuilding Syria is a top state priority and emphasised the need for international support.<ref name="SANA">{{Cite web |date=13 October 2025 |title=President al-Sharaa to CBS News: Reconstruction Could Cost Up to $900 Billion - Syrian Arab News Agency (SANA) |url=https://sana.sy/en/politics/2272265/ |access-date=13 October 2025 |language=en-US |archive-date=13 October 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251013225115/https://sana.sy/en/politics/2272265/ |url-status=live }}</ref> He said Syria has reshaped its political identity after years of isolation and is now pursuing balanced relations with the [[Western world|West]], the United States, and regional powers such as Saudi Arabia and Turkey, adding that its calm and stable ties with Russia and China, built on shared strategic interests, align rather than conflict with its relations with Western nations.<ref>{{Cite web |date=16 October 2025 |title=President al-Sharaa to CBS News: Syria"s liberation Ended its International Isolation - Syrian Arab News Agency (SANA) |url=https://sana.sy/en/presidency/2272634/ |access-date=17 October 2025 |language=en-US |archive-date=4 November 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251104093838/https://sana.sy/en/presidency/2272634/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

In a November 2025 interview with ''[[Washington Post|The Washington Post]]'', al-Sharaa said Syria is emerging from a long war and decades of dictatorship and is in a transitional phase. He compared the country's rebuilding to the long recovery after the [[American Civil War]]. He also said that some groups push their own interests and seek autonomy by claiming threats to their sect or creed, adding, "In Syria, we have been living in coexistence with different groups and different religious communities for 1,400 years. We're still here, and we still have that diversity."<ref name="SANA12">{{Cite web |date=12 November 2025 |title=President al-Sharaa: We seek a balanced partnership with Washington – Interview - Syrian Arab News Agency (SANA) |url=https://sana.sy/en/politics/2277361/ |access-date=15 November 2025 |language=en-US |archive-date=13 November 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251113090621/https://sana.sy/en/politics/2277361/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

On 6 December 2025, at the [[Doha Forum]], al-Sharaa told CNN that Syria was moving towards stability and economic recovery following the revolution and the transition to a new system of governance. He noted that his government was working with the United States to lift the [[Caesar Syria Civilian Protection Act|Caesar Act]] and explained that the [[Syrian National Dialogue Conference|National Dialogue Conference]] held after the liberation had resulted in a [[Constitutional Declaration of the Syrian Arab Republic|provisional constitution]] granting him a five-year mandate to pass new laws before a new permanent constitution was drafted ahead of elections scheduled in four years. He spoke about [[Civil and political rights|civil rights]], stating that Syria had closed oppressive prisons such as [[Sednaya Prison]] and was committed to [[women's empowerment]], with women fully participating in society, government, and the [[People's Assembly of Syria|People's Assembly]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=6 December 2025 |title=President al-Sharaa outlines Syria's path toward stability, rebuilding, and recovery |url=https://sana.sy/en/presidency/2281990/ |access-date=7 December 2025 |language=en-US |archive-date=6 December 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251206165404/https://sana.sy/en/presidency/2281990/ |url-status=live }}</ref> He also spoke about engaging with [[Alawites]], [[Druze]], and other minorities, stressing that every sector of Syrian society had suffered under the Assad regime and that all of them took part in the revolution that brought it down. Al-Sharaa highlighted the need to uphold the rule of law in Syria, saying that it is "the way to guarantee everybody's rights, including the rights of all minorities".<ref>{{Cite web |author=jcookson |date=19 December 2025 |title=Dispatch from Riyadh: Why Syria is central to the Middle East's future |url=https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/content-series/inflection-points/dispatch-from-riyadh-why-syria-is-central-to-the-middle-easts-future/ |access-date=19 December 2025 |website=Atlantic Council |language=en-US |archive-date=19 December 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251219123724/https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/content-series/inflection-points/dispatch-from-riyadh-why-syria-is-central-to-the-middle-easts-future/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

On 10 January 2026, al-Sharaa said in an interview with ''Shams TV'' that the government was engaged in dialogue with the [[Syrian Democratic Forces]] (SDF). He said that the [[Syrian peace process#10 March agreement|10 March agreement]] protects Kurdish rights and culture, strengthens state authority across Syria, and has wide regional and international support. Al-Sharaa stressed that the [[Kurds|Kurdish]] community should be protected through integration into the Syrian state, not through armed groups, with rights guaranteed by the constitution and participation based on ability. He reaffirmed Syria's commitment to the agreement and called on the SDF to integrate, stating that unity and the rule of law are essential for Syria's stability.<ref name=":4">{{Cite web |date=15 January 2026 |title=President al-Sharaa: Kurdish rights guaranteed by constitution |url=https://sana.sy/en/syria/2290518/ |access-date=15 January 2026 |language=en-US |archive-date=14 January 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260114213439/https://sana.sy/en/syria/2290518/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Al-Sharaa said that in their first meeting, he told SDF general commander [[Mazloum Abdi]] that if the goal was to secure Kurdish rights, there was no need for bloodshed, as those rights would be guaranteed by the constitution.<ref name=":4" /> He stated that [[Democratic Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria|North and East Syria]] holds most of Syria's main resources, including oil, gas, water, agriculture, and energy. He added that SDF control had blocked the state from accessing these resources, damaging the economy and slowing reconstruction, despite the urgent need to improve living conditions.<ref name=":4" />

In a September 2025 interview with ''[[60 Minutes]]'', al-Sharaa said Syria poses no threat to Israel and denounced Israeli strikes on the People's Palace as "a declaration of war". He warned that Israel's provocations could drag Syria into war and push U.S. allies to seek alternatives. He urged Israel to withdraw from areas seized after 8 December 2024, and said Syria has made no provocations or threats toward Israel. He added that "the [[Thirty-seventh government of Israel|current Israeli government]] is [[Extremism|extremist]] and guided by [[Expansionism|expansionist]] doctrines," believing that "Israel has the right to establish a larger state than it is now and seeks to expand into Gaza and the [[West Bank]], then north and south." He also said Israel refuses to allow the return of [[United Nations Disengagement Observer Force|UN forces]] to the 1974 disengagement lines and is trying to impose "a new format for the security arrangement." He emphasised that Syria seeks a peaceful solution protecting its [[territorial integrity]] and rightful claim to the [[Occupied Golan Heights|occupied Golan heights]], warning that Israel's expansionist ambitions could impact Jordan, Iraq, Turkey, and Egypt, and reshape regional alliances.<ref name="SANA" /><ref>{{Cite web |date=15 October 2025 |title=Al-Sharaa: The Golan is Syrian territory, and we are trying to regain it through negotiations |url=https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2025/10/al-sharaa-the-golan-is-syrian-territory-and-we-are-trying-to-regain-it-through-negotiations/ |access-date=16 October 2025 |website=Enab Baladi |language=en-US |archive-date=18 October 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251018114416/https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2025/10/al-sharaa-the-golan-is-syrian-territory-and-we-are-trying-to-regain-it-through-negotiations/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

=== Views on Iran ===
Since the fall of the Assad regime, al-Sharaa has made several statements regarding [[Iranian intervention in the Syrian civil war|Iran's involvement in Syria]]. For many years, Syria and Iran maintained a strategic alliance, with Damascus serving as a key component of Iran's "[[Axis of Resistance]]".<ref>{{Cite web |date=13 January 2025 |title=The Fall of Syria and Iran's Strategy of Relying on Allies – Manara Magazine |url=https://manaramagazine.org/2025/01/the-fall-of-syria-and-irans-strategy-of-relying-on-allies/ |access-date=2 February 2025 |language=en-GB |archive-date=2 February 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250202154617/https://manaramagazine.org/2025/01/the-fall-of-syria-and-irans-strategy-of-relying-on-allies/ |url-status=live }}</ref> In an interview with ''[[Asharq Al-Awsat]]'' published on 20 December 2024, al-Sharaa asserted that under Bashar al-Assad, Syria became a platform for Iran to exert influence over major Arab capitals, expand conflicts, and destabilise [[Arab states of the Persian Gulf|Gulf nations]] through activities such as drug trafficking, including the [[Ba'athist Syrian Captagon industry|distribution]] of [[Captagon]]. He described Iran's regional ambitions as detrimental and framed the developments in Syria as a setback for Iran's influence in the region. "What we have done and achieved with the least possible damage and losses," he stated, adding that "the Iranian project in the region has been set back 40 years."<ref>{{Cite web |date=20 December 2024 |title=Al-Sharaa to Asharq Al-Awsat: Revolution Ended with Regime's Fall, Will Not Be Exported |url=https://english.aawsat.com/interviews/5093482-al-sharaa-asharq-al-awsat-revolution-ended-regime%E2%80%99s-fall-will-not-be-exported |access-date=2 February 2025 |website=english.aawsat.com |language=en |archive-date=30 January 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250130165435/https://english.aawsat.com/interviews/5093482-al-sharaa-asharq-al-awsat-revolution-ended-regime%E2%80%99s-fall-will-not-be-exported |url-status=live }}</ref> In February 2025, al-Sharaa condemned Iran and its "Axis of Resistance" as a "strategic threat to the entire region".<ref>{{Cite news |last=Myre |first=Greg |date=4 February 2025 |title=Syria's new leader denounces Iran, calling its proxies a regional threat |url=https://www.npr.org/2025/02/04/g-s1-46361/syrias-new-leader-denounces-iran-calling-its-proxies-a-regional-threat |access-date=26 May 2025 |work=NPR |language=en |archive-date=18 May 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250518173616/https://www.npr.org/2025/02/04/g-s1-46361/syrias-new-leader-denounces-iran-calling-its-proxies-a-regional-threat |url-status=live }}</ref>

== Documentary ==
On 1 June 2021, [[Frontline (American TV program)|PBS ''Frontline'']] released a documentary, ''The Jihadist'', investigating al-Sharaa's past in the context of the ongoing Syrian civil war.<ref>{{Cite AV media|title=The Designated Terrorist and the Fight Over the Future of Syria | date=June 2021 |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0-vbHHl8RpY|publisher=PBS|language=en|access-date=4 June 2021|archive-date=4 June 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210604211458/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0-vbHHl8RpY|url-status=live}}</ref> In the interview, reflecting on his past affiliation with al-Qaeda, [[United States foreign policy in the Middle East|U.S. foreign policy in the Middle East]] and the [[Palestinian nationalism|Palestinian cause]], al-Sharaa commented:<blockquote>The history of the region and what it went through over the past 20 or 30 years needs to be taken into consideration... We are talking about a region ruled by tyrants, by people who rule with iron fists and their security apparatuses. At the same time, this region is surrounded by numerous conflicts and wars... We can't take a segment of this history and say so-and-so joined al-Qaeda. There are thousands of people who joined al-Qaeda, but let us ask what was the reason behind these people joining al-Qaeda? That's the question. Are the U.S. policies after World War II toward the region partially responsibility for driving people towards al-Qaeda? And are the European policies in the region responsible for the reactions of people who sympathise with the Palestinian cause or with the way the Zionist regime deals with the Palestinians?... Are the broken and oppressed peoples who had to endure what happened in Iraq, for example, or in Afghanistan, are they responsible?... Our involvement with al-Qaeda in the past was an era, and it ended, and even at that time when we were with al-Qaeda, we were against external attacks, and it's completely against our policies to carry out external operations from Syria to target European or American people. This was not part of our calculations at all, and we did not do it at all.<ref name= frontline/></blockquote>
